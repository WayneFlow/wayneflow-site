Key takeaways
  • In the outsourced appointment setting vs in-house SDR decision, salary is the smallest line. A single U.S. SDR at median pay costs roughly $114,000 to $123,000 in year one once you add benefits, tools, recruiting and management time to the $80,000 median OTE.
  • At the median quota of 10 held meetings a month, that works out to about $1,170 per held meeting in year one, and about $1,550 if the rep hits 70% of quota.
  • Time is the bigger gap: hiring plus ramp puts a new SDR at full speed roughly four to five months after you open the role. A well-run agency campaign can get replies in the first week after launch.
  • In-house wins when deals are complex, accounts are few, or you are building a future AE bench. Agencies win on speed, infrastructure and predictable cost per meeting. Many teams should run both.

Most comparisons of outsourced appointment setting vs in-house SDR stop at salary. That is rarely where the real money goes. The real cost of an SDR includes payroll taxes, benefits, a data and email stack, a recruiter's time, a manager's time, three months of ramp, and the very real chance you do it all again in 18 months.

This guide puts every line item on the table for a U.S. company in 2026, with sources. Then it turns those costs into the only number that matters, cost per qualified meeting, and shows how to compare that against an appointment setting agency quote.

We run cold email and LinkedIn outreach for B2B companies every day, so we will use our own campaign data where it helps. Where we cite industry numbers, we link the source and note its year.

What an in-house SDR really costs in 2026

Let's build the fully loaded cost of hiring an SDR one line at a time. We use medians, not best cases, and we show the assumption behind every number so you can swap in your own.

Base salary and OTE

The most useful benchmark is The Bridge Group's 2025 SDR Metrics and Compensation report, a survey of 351 B2B companies. It puts median SDR on-target earnings at $80,000, split 68:32 into a $55,000 base and $25,000 variable. The report notes OTE has not moved since 2022.

Self-reported data lands in the same neighborhood. RepVue's SDR salary page (data as of December 2025) shows a median base of about $60,000 and median OTE of about $85,000. Expect more in New York, San Francisco and well-funded SaaS companies, and less in lower-cost regions.

For our worked example we use the Bridge Group figures: $55,000 base plus $25,000 variable at target.

Benefits and payroll taxes

Salary is not what an employee costs you. The Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks this precisely. In its Employer Costs for Employee Compensation data for June 2026, private-industry employers paid $26.99 an hour in wages for sales and related workers, and benefits made up 24.7% of total compensation.

Some of that benefit cost is paid leave, which is already inside a salaried rep's pay. Strip out paid leave (5.9% of compensation) and the remaining insurance, retirement, payroll taxes and other benefits come to about 25 cents for every dollar of wages. That is the load we use.

On $80,000 of cash compensation, a 25% load adds $20,000.

Tools: data, sequencing and email infrastructure

An SDR without a stack is a person with a phone. Here is a lean, entry-level outbound stack using public list prices as of October 2026:

  • Prospecting data: Apollo Professional lists at $79 per seat per month billed annually, or $948 a year.
  • LinkedIn prospecting: LinkedIn Sales Navigator Core lists at $1,079.88 per year billed annually ($119.99 a month if billed monthly).
  • Email sequencing: Instantly's Hypergrowth plan lists at $97 a month on monthly billing, or $1,164 a year.
  • Sending inboxes: Google Workspace Business Starter lists at $7 per user per month on an annual plan. Five sending inboxes run $420 a year.

That totals about $3,600 a year, and it is a floor. It leaves out the CRM, a dialer, mobile number data, intent data, extra sending domains, email verification and enterprise data platforms that do not publish prices. Many teams spend well beyond this.

Recruiting and management time

SHRM reported in 2022 that the average cost per hire was nearly $4,700. That number is a few years old, so treat it as a conservative floor. It also excludes the hours your sales leader spends screening and interviewing, and any agency recruiter fee.

Management is the cost founders forget. The BLS lists the median pay for sales managers at $148,270 (May 2025). If coaching, 1:1s, call reviews, list QA and copy edits take about 10% of a manager's time, that is roughly $14,800 of salary aimed at one SDR. In a founder-led company, that time comes out of the founder's week instead.

Ramp time and turnover: the costs you do not see on an invoice

The Bridge Group's 2025 data puts average SDR ramp at 3.0 months and average tenure at 1.9 years. Median annual SDR attrition was 40% in 2024, though part of that is healthy promotion into closing roles.

Put those together and the picture gets clear. A rep who stays 1.9 years spends roughly three of those months ramping. Then the seat often sits empty while you hire again, and the next rep ramps from zero. SHRM's 2026 recruiting benchmarking puts median time-to-fill at 39 calendar days for nonexecutive roles. Every vacancy is more than a month of zero meetings, plus three months of partial ones.

Performance is not guaranteed either. The Bridge Group found only 60% of SDRs hit quota in its 2025 study, the lowest in the report's history. RepVue's data shows a similar 57%.

How appointment setting agencies price their work

Lead generation agency pricing varies too much, and too little of it is published, to give you an honest "typical range." Quotes depend on your market, the size of your target list, the channels involved and how strictly a meeting must be qualified. So instead of quoting a range we cannot verify, here is how to read the models you will see:

  • Monthly retainer. A flat fee for a defined scope (channels, volume, list building). Predictable to budget, but you carry the performance risk unless there is a guarantee.
  • Pay per meeting. You pay for each booked or held meeting. Aligned on paper, but read the fine print on what counts. "Booked" and "held" are very different numbers.
  • Hybrid. A smaller base fee plus a per-meeting fee or a guaranteed minimum. Common, and often the fairest split of risk.
  • Setup fees. Some agencies charge separately for domains, inboxes, warm-up and list building. Ask whether these are included.

Whatever the model, convert every quote into the same unit: total monthly cost divided by qualified meetings held. That is the only fair way to compare an outsourced SDR against a hire.

Cost per appointment: the math that settles the debate

The formula is simple:

Cost per held meeting = Total cost for the period ÷ Qualified meetings held in that period

The hard part is being honest about both sides of the fraction.

Worked example: the in-house side

The Bridge Group's 2025 report puts the median SDR quota at 10 held first meetings a month. Let's give our rep a generous year one:

  1. Months 1 to 3 (ramp): assume half of full output, or 1.5 months of full-quota production.
  2. Months 4 to 12: full quota, 9 months.
  3. Total: 10.5 full-quota months × 10 meetings = 105 held meetings at target.

Now divide:

  • At target: $123,139 ÷ 105 = about $1,170 per held meeting.
  • At 70% of quota (73.5 meetings): $113,764 ÷ 73.5 = about $1,550 per held meeting.

Notice what this example assumes: the rep is hired on day one, never leaves, and the tool stack is the cheapest workable one. With 40% median attrition, a real year often looks worse.

Worked example: the agency side

Now flip it into a break-even test. If an agency commits to 25 qualified, held meetings a month, multiply by your in-house cost per meeting:

  • 25 × $1,170 = about $29,300 a month is the point where the agency matches a median rep at full quota.
  • 25 × $1,550 = about $38,700 a month is the break-even against a rep at 70% of quota.

Any quote below your break-even line beats the in-house option on cost per appointment, as long as "qualified" means the same thing on both sides. Write that definition down: title, company size, industry, and that the prospect agreed to a specific conversation about your offer.

Time to first meeting: in-house vs outsourced

Cost per meeting is half the story. The other half is when meetings start. A quarter of lost pipeline can matter more than the price difference.

Typical in-house timeline

  1. Open the role to signed offer: median 39 days, per SHRM's 2026 data.
  2. Notice period and start date: often two weeks or more for an employed candidate.
  3. Onboarding, tool setup and inbox warm-up: new sending domains need time before they can carry real volume.
  4. Ramp to full quota: 3.0 months on average, per The Bridge Group.

Add it up and a new SDR usually reaches full output four to five months after you decide to hire. That is a long time to wait if the board wants pipeline this quarter.

Typical outsourced timeline

An agency that already has the systems skips the hiring and most of the ramp. Our own four-step appointment setting process takes about three weeks of setup: ICP and offer work, list building and verification, separate secondary domains, inbox warm-up and copy approval. Replies typically start in the first week after launch.

From our dashboards

Our 5-day blitz from September 7 to 11, 2026 sent 96,408 emails to 79,587 contacts with a 0.45% bounce rate. It produced 1,095 replies, 115 interested prospects and 25 calls booked in one week, representing $62,500 in pipeline at the client's average deal size.

One week is not a promise for every market. A niche with 2,000 total buyers will move slower than one with 80,000. But the gap in months is structural, not luck.

The risks of each model

Neither option is risk-free. Here is an honest list of what can go wrong, and how to guard against it.

In-house SDR risks

  • Bad hire. You may not know for three to four months, after ramp, whether the rep can do the job.
  • Turnover. With 40% median attrition, plan on replacing the seat. Each exit resets ramp and costs another round of recruiting.
  • Deliverability. Many reps send from the company's main domain. If spam complaints pile up, it can hurt deliverability for your whole team, including invoices and customer email.
  • Management gap. An unmanaged SDR drifts. Without a playbook, coaching and list QA, activity goes up while meetings stay flat.
  • Single point of failure. Vacation, sickness or a resignation takes pipeline to zero.

Agency risks

  • Domain reputation. A careless vendor can burn domains. Require separate secondary sending domains so your main domain is never touched.
  • Off-brand messaging. Approve every sequence before it goes live, and review replies weekly.
  • Loose qualification. "Meeting booked" can mean a student who clicked a link. Define a qualified meeting in writing, and pay for or measure held meetings.
  • Lock-in. Watch for long minimum terms with no performance exit.
  • Black-box reporting. Ask for live dashboard access, not a monthly PDF. Our case studies with live campaign dashboards show the level of transparency worth asking for from any vendor.
  • Knowledge leaving with the vendor. Your contract should say you own the lists, copy and reply data.

When an in-house SDR is the right call

We sell appointment setting, and we will still tell you that hiring is the better choice in several situations:

  • Complex, technical first calls. If the first meeting needs real discovery on architecture, compliance or integrations, a rep steeped in your product earns their cost.
  • A small, named account list. With 300 target accounts, you need multi-threading, research and patience, not volume.
  • Heavy inbound. If marketing already generates more leads than your AEs can follow up, a rep who qualifies inbound fast may beat any outbound program.
  • Building a future AE bench. The Bridge Group counts promotions as 16 percentage points of its 40% attrition figure. If you want to grow closers from within, the SDR seat is a training ground.
  • A proven playbook and a manager with time. If you already know your ICP, messaging and conversion rates, a new rep has a much better shot at hitting quota.

If three or more of those describe you, hiring may well be the smarter path. If none do, the math above probably favors outsourcing, at least for now.

The hybrid model: outsourced SDR plus in-house closers

For many U.S. B2B companies the answer is not either-or. The hybrid model splits the work by what each side does best:

  • Agency: list building, verification, sending infrastructure, high-volume cold email and LinkedIn outreach, and booking first meetings.
  • In-house: handling positive replies that need product depth, phone follow-up, inbound leads, strategic accounts and closing.

It also works as a sequence. Use an agency to test a new vertical or region for one or two quarters. If a segment converts, you have the data to justify a hire and a proven message for that rep to start with.

LinkedIn is a good example of where the split helps. In our own LinkedIn campaigns to founders and angel investors, 1,375 invites produced 351 accepted connections, and 1,628 InMails produced 154 replies. Warm replies like those are exactly where an in-house expert adds value.

Decision checklist: outsource, hire or both?

Work through these in order. Write down your answers, and the right model usually becomes obvious.

  1. How soon do you need pipeline? If the answer is this quarter, hiring alone will probably miss.
  2. What is your fully loaded in-house cost? Rebuild the year-one table above with your real salary band, benefits and tools.
  3. What is your realistic meetings-per-month number? Use the median quota of 10 held meetings unless you have your own data.
  4. What is your break-even agency price? Multiply your in-house cost per held meeting by the meetings an agency commits to.
  5. Who will manage the SDR? Name the person and the hours per week. If nobody has the time, count that as a risk.
  6. How complex is the first call? The more technical discovery it needs, the more in-house makes sense.
  7. How large is your addressable list? Tens of thousands of contacts favors volume outreach. A few hundred accounts favors a dedicated rep.
  8. Can you protect your domain? Whoever runs outreach should use separate sending domains, verified lists and business-hours sending.
  9. What does "qualified" mean? Agree on title, company size, industry and intent before anyone books a meeting.
  10. What is your exit plan? For a hire, it is a replacement plan. For an agency, it is contract terms and data ownership.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to hire an SDR in 2026?

For a U.S. company, plan on roughly $114,000 to $123,000 in year one for a single SDR. That covers median OTE of about $80,000 (The Bridge Group, 2025), a 25% benefits and payroll-tax load, a basic tool stack near $3,600, recruiting near $4,700 and a slice of a manager's time. High-cost metros and venture-backed tech companies often pay more.

What is a good cost per appointment for B2B?

There is no universal number, because it depends on deal size and how "qualified" is defined. A practical ceiling is what your in-house alternative costs. In our model, a median SDR produces held meetings at about $1,170 each in year one, rising to about $1,550 at 70% of quota. Compare any quote against that, using meetings held, not just booked.

How long does it take an in-house SDR to start booking meetings?

Longer than most budgets assume. SHRM's 2026 benchmarking puts median time-to-fill at 39 calendar days for nonexecutive roles, and The Bridge Group's 2025 report puts average SDR ramp at 3.0 months. Add a notice period, onboarding and inbox warm-up, and full productivity usually lands four to five months after you open the role.

Is outsourced appointment setting worth it for small companies?

Often, yes, if you do not have a sales leader with time to manage an SDR or you need meetings within a quarter. An agency brings the data, tooling and copywriting that a single new hire would have to build from scratch. It is a weaker fit if your first call needs deep technical discovery or your market is only a few hundred named accounts.

What are the risks of hiring an appointment setting agency?

The main risks are damage to your domain reputation, off-brand messaging, a loose definition of "qualified," and long lock-in contracts. Protect yourself by requiring separate sending domains, approving copy before launch, defining a qualified meeting in writing, getting live dashboard access and keeping ownership of your lead lists and reply data.

Can you combine an outsourced SDR with an in-house team?

Yes, and it is often the best answer. A common split has the agency running high-volume cold email and LinkedIn outreach to open conversations, while your in-house rep or account executive handles positive replies, phone follow-up, inbound leads and strategic accounts. You get speed now and keep product knowledge and relationships inside the company.
