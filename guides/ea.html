Why emailing the CEO fails

Many CEOs don't read their own inbox. An assistant sorts it first into three piles: for the boss, for someone else, or delete. A sales pitch goes straight to delete.

The 2-line email

Email to the assistant or CEO
Subject: quick question

Hi {first_name}, who owns [your topic, e.g. sales hiring / outbound] at {company} these days?
Happy to reach out to them directly.
How we use this at WayneFlow

We aim every campaign at the real decision makers, not just the biggest title. For one construction software client that meant 31 qualified demos and 6 annual contracts. Finding the right person at each company is slow research work, and it's a big part of what we do for clients.

The follow-up email to the person they name

Email to the right person
Subject: {assistant_first_name} pointed me to you

Hi {first_name}, {assistant_first_name} said you're the right person for [topic].
[One line on the result you get for companies like theirs.]
Worth a quick chat?

The rules

  • ✅ Ask for a name. It's easy to answer.
  • ❌ Never ask for the boss's calendar.
  • ⚡ Email the person they point to the same day, while the name is fresh.
Pro tip: Now your email opens with "Sarah said you're the right person", and it isn't cold anymore.

Quick answers

What if nobody replies?

Send one short follow-up after 3 days. If there's still no answer, move to the next company.

Is it okay to mention the assistant's name?

Yes, as long as it's true: only use the name of the person who actually pointed you there.
