Why warm-up feels slow and still matters

A brand-new inbox has no sending history. Email providers like Gmail and Outlook don't know yet if you're a real person or a spammer. If that inbox suddenly sends cold emails to strangers, it looks exactly like a spammer, and your emails land in spam. Nobody sees your offer.

Warm-up fixes this. For 14 days your new inboxes swap normal emails with other real inboxes in a warm-up network. Those emails get opened, replied to and moved out of spam. The providers learn that your inboxes behave like real people. Then you start your campaign slowly.

This isn't just a cold email myth. Google's email sender guidelines tell senders to increase sending volume slowly, to start with a low volume and to avoid sudden spikes when you have no sending history. Instantly's warm-up guide says an account should be warmed up for at least 2 weeks before campaigns.

Part 1: Before day 1 (setup)

Why it matters: warm-up builds a good name for a domain and its inboxes. If the setup is broken, you spend 14 days warming something that will still fail. Fix the basics first, then start the clock.

  1. Buy 2 or more separate sending domains that look like your brand (for acme.com: getacme.com, tryacme.com). Never warm up or cold email from your main domain. Our main domain guide shows the full safe setup.
  2. Point each new domain to your real website, so anyone who checks it lands somewhere real.
  3. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC to every sending domain. Google requires SPF or DKIM for all senders, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC for senders of 5,000+ emails a day to Gmail (Google sender guidelines).
  4. Check the MX records are correct, so replies actually reach your inboxes.
  5. Create a few inboxes per domain (we use 3), each with a real first name (anna@getacme.com), a profile photo and a simple signature.
  6. Buy 1 or 2 spare domains now and warm them up too, so you can swap one in later without waiting another 14 days.
Quick self-test: Send one normal email from each new inbox to your own Gmail and open "Show original". SPF, DKIM and DMARC should all say PASS. If one says FAIL, fix it before you start warm-up.

Part 2: Day 1 (turn it on)

  1. Connect every inbox to your sending tool (for example Instantly).
  2. Turn warm-up on for every inbox on the same day.
  3. Keep the slow warm-up setting. Instantly's guide says to stick to 10 warm-up emails a day for new accounts and to never turn on "Disable slow warmup" for new accounts.
  4. Write down day 1 in your calendar and set a reminder for day 14.

Part 3: Days 1 to 14 (wait, but don't waste it)

Why it matters: the rule is simple, zero cold emails for 14 days. Not even "just a few to test". A few early cold emails can undo the trust warm-up is building. But the waiting time is not lost time. Use it to get everything else ready, so day 15 is a clean start.

  • Build your lead list. Pick one clear type of buyer and one problem you solve for them.
  • Verify every email address. Remove invalid and risky ones. Bounces hurt a fresh inbox fast.
  • Write your emails. One short first email and 2 or 3 follow-ups.
  • Set up Google Postmaster Tools for each sending domain, so you can see your spam rate later.
  • Check warm-up once a week (day 7). Make sure it's running on every inbox and nothing is disconnected.

Part 5: Day 15 and on (start slowly)

Why it matters: Google's guidelines warn that suddenly doubling your volume can lead to rate limits or a drop in reputation, and they say to send at a consistent rate instead of in bursts. So the first campaign starts small and grows step by step.

  1. Week 3: about 10 cold emails per inbox per day.
  2. Week 4: step up to 20, only if the weekly check is green.
  3. Week 5 and on: up to 30 per inbox per day. Don't go higher per inbox; add inboxes instead.
  4. Send a similar amount every working day. No big bursts.
  5. Keep warm-up on the whole time.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping warm-up because "the domain is new, so it's clean". New is exactly the problem.
  • Sending "just a few" cold emails during the 14 days.
  • Turning off slow warm-up to go faster.
  • Starting warm-up before SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set.
  • Going from 0 to 100 emails per inbox on day 15.
  • Turning warm-up off once the campaign starts.

Quick answers

Can I warm up for 7 days instead of 14?

You can, but you take a bigger risk. Instantly recommends at least 2 weeks before campaigns. A week saved is not worth a burned domain.

Do I need to warm up if my domain is old?

If the inbox is new or has never sent cold email, yes. Warm-up builds history for the inbox and its sending pattern, not just the domain's age.

Should I turn warm-up off once the campaign starts?

No. Keep it on. It keeps a steady stream of good activity on each inbox while you send cold email.

Can I warm up my main business email?

Don't send cold email from your main domain at all. Use separate sending domains, and keep your main domain for clients and invoices.
