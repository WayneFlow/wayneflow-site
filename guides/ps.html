Why the P.S. gets read

People skim cold emails. They read the first line, jump around, and their eyes drop to the bottom to see how it ends. A P.S. sits alone, after your name, so it stands out from the rest of the email.

That makes it the best spot for the one thing that makes a stranger believe you: proof. And because almost nobody writes one, a good P.S. also makes your email look different from the 50 others in their inbox.

The 4 kinds of P.S. that work

TypeWhy it worksUse it when
Proof from their worldA dentist trusts what you did for a dentist more than what you did for a software companyYou have a result in their industry

Honesty first: Only use real results. Ask before you name a client. If you don't have numbers yet, use the free-offer lines instead of making something up.

ODD, EXACT NUMBERS

5. P.S. [Client type] went from [4] to [11] booked calls a month in [90] days.

SOMETHING FREE (NO PROOF YET)

7. P.S. I made a 2-minute video with 3 ideas for [company]'s [website]. Want me to send it?
8. P.S. Happy to send the exact email template I'd use for [their market], free.

SOFT CLOSE
9. P.S. Not the right time? Reply "later" and I'll check back next quarter.
10. P.S. If this isn't your area, who's the best person to ask at [company]?

How to write your own

  1. Pick the reader's world. Industry, role, size. The P.S. should name their world, not yours.
  2. Find your closest proof. Same industry beats same size. Same size beats no proof.
  3. Use the exact number. If the real number is 137, write 137. Don't round it to "100+".
  4. No proof? Offer something free that's useful on its own: a checklist, a short video, a template.
  5. Keep it to one line. If it needs two, the proof is too complicated. Pick a simpler one.
  6. Read it alone. Cover the rest of the email. Does the P.S. still make sense and make you curious?

Write 6 P.S. lines for this list:
- 2 using proof from their world (closest industry match first)
- 2 with exact numbers from my results
- 1 free offer they can say yes to without a call
- 1 soft close

Rules: one line each, under 20 words, start with "P.S.", plain words, no hype, no exclamation marks. If I have no result that fits this industry, say so and give me 2 extra free-offer lines instead.
The rule: One line. If your P.S. needs a second line, it's a second email.

Quick answers

Does every cold email need a P.S.?

No, but it's a free place to add proof. If you have nothing useful to say there, leave it out. An empty "P.S. Let me know!" adds nothing.

What if I don't have any results yet?

Use the free-offer lines: a checklist, a short video or a template. They give the reader a reason to reply without needing proof.

Can I mention a client by name?

Only with their permission. Otherwise describe them: "a dental clinic in Texas" works almost as well.

Should the P.S. go after my signature?

Yes. Name first, then the P.S. on its own line at the very bottom. If you use an opt-out line, put it under the P.S.
