Why the juggle always breaks

Most founders of small agencies and service businesses do three jobs: sales, delivery and admin. Each one is fine alone. Together, something always slips. Usually it's sales, because delivery has a deadline and admin has a bill. Sales only has a "later".

This is not just a founder problem. Salesforce's State of Sales research found that sales reps spend just 28% of their week actually selling. The rest goes to other tasks. If full-time sales people lose most of their week to busywork, a founder who also delivers the work has even less time left.

The fix from the reel: drop sales. Not forever. Just the part that eats your week. The part that needs you (talking to buyers and closing) stays with you. The part that doesn't (lists, inboxes, sending, sorting replies) goes to someone else.

JobDoes it need you?Best move
DeliveryYes: it's what clients pay you forKeep
Closing sales callsYes: buyers want to meet the person who does the workKeep
Building lists, inboxes, sending, sorting repliesNo: it's a repeatable processHand off
Admin (invoices, scheduling)Mostly noHand off or use tools (not covered here)

These are the 5 parts from the reel. Each one is real work, and each one repeats every week. That's why they're the right ones to hand off: once written down, someone else can do them the same way every time.

Step 1: Write your ideal client on one page

Why it works: the person building your list can't read your mind. If you say "small businesses", you get a random list and bad replies. If you say "dental groups with 3 to 10 locations where the owner signs", you get a list that looks like your best clients. One clear page saves weeks of wrong emails.

  1. List your last 10 clients. Mark your 3 favorites (paid well, easy to work with, got great results).
  2. For each favorite write: industry, company size, location, the job title of the person who signed, and what was happening when they hired you.
  3. Write down who you don't want (too small, wrong country, bad fit). This list matters as much as the first one.
  4. Use the hand-off brief prompt below to turn it into one clean page.

Step 2: Write your offer and one proof

Why it works: you know your business best. The person sending the emails can write well, but the offer and the proof must come from you. A cold email with no proof asks a stranger to trust you for no reason.

  1. Write what you do in one sentence a 10-year-old would understand.
  2. Add one real result from a real client, with a number. Ask the client first if you name them.
  3. Pick a small ask, like "Worth a look?" instead of "Can we book 30 minutes?".

Step 3: Approve separate sending domains and inboxes

Why it works: your main domain is where clients, invoices and referrals reach you. Never risk it with cold email. Sending happens from separate domains that look like your brand (for acme.com: getacme.com or acmehq.com). Google's sender guidelines require SPF or DKIM for every sender, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC for bulk senders, so ask for all three from day one.

  1. Approve the domain names before they're bought. They should look like your brand, not spam.
  2. Ask for up to 3 inboxes per domain, under real names from your team.
  3. Ask for proof that SPF, DKIM and DMARC are set up on every domain. Our deliverability checklist shows the checks.
  4. Make sure the domains and inboxes are in your name and on your account, so you keep them if you ever switch who runs the outbound.
Why about 30 emails a day per inbox: Google Workspace allows up to 2,000 emails a day per user (Google's limits), but that's a system limit, not a target. Hunter's State of Email Outreach found accounts sending 20 to 49 emails a day got 5.7% replies vs 4.5% overall. Need more volume? Add inboxes, not emails per inbox.

Step 4: Let the inboxes warm up while the list is built

Why it works: new inboxes have no history, so email providers don't trust them yet. Instantly's warm-up guide says accounts should be warmed up for at least 2 weeks. Those 2 weeks are not wasted: that's when the list gets built and checked.

  1. Week 1 to 2: inboxes warm up automatically. Nobody sends campaigns yet.
  2. Same 2 weeks: the list is built from your Step 1 page, and every email is verified.
  3. Ask for 1 or 2 people per company, not 5. Hunter found that contacting one or two people per company got 5.1% replies vs 3.5% for 3 or more.
  4. Look at a sample of 20 rows before sending starts. If 5 of them aren't companies you'd want, fix the Step 1 page.

Why it works: if you have to approve every email, you're still juggling. Approve the first sequence once, set a few rules, then let it run. Change it only when the weekly numbers say so.

  1. Ask for a short sequence: 3 emails, each under about 100 words, plain text, no images.
  2. Check 3 things only: is the offer right, is the proof true, does it sound like you?
  3. Agree on one rule for changes: new copy gets tested on a small part of the list first.

Step 6: Write reply rules

Why it works: replies are where most of the hidden time goes. Most replies are not buyers: out-of-office notes, "not now", "remove me", wrong person. Someone else can handle all of those. You only want the people who want to talk. And they should hear back fast: research published in Harvard Business Review found most companies respond to leads far too slowly.

Step 7: Block closing time on your calendar

Why it works: if anyone can book any slot, sales calls land in the middle of delivery work and the juggle starts again. Fixed closing blocks protect both.

  1. Pick 2 or 3 fixed blocks a week for sales calls (for example Tuesday and Thursday afternoons).
  2. Make your booking link show only those blocks.
  3. Ask for a short note before each call: who they are, what they replied, what they asked. Read it in 2 minutes before the call.

Step 8: Review a 15-minute scorecard every Monday

Why it works: handing off doesn't mean looking away. A few numbers, once a week, tell you if it's working. If they're fine, you're done for the week. If one drops, you know exactly where to look.

Worked example: what lands on your plate

Here are real numbers from a small pilot we ran (case 05 on our results page): 10,284 people contacted, 417 replies, 103 sales opportunities.

  • Reply rate: 417 ÷ 10,284 = about 4%.
  • Opportunities per reply: 103 ÷ 417 = about 1 in 4 replies (24.7%).
  • Opportunities per person contacted: 103 ÷ 10,284 = about 1 in 100.
  • Without a hand-off, you read and sort all 417 replies yourself. With one, you only see the 103 that want to talk. About 314 replies (75%) never reach you.

Now the setup side. Say you want to reach the same 10,284 people over 4 weeks (20 working days) with first emails:

  • First emails a day: 10,284 ÷ 20 = about 514.
  • Inboxes at about 30 a day: 514 ÷ 30 = 17.1, so 18 inboxes.
  • Domains at 3 inboxes each: 18 ÷ 3 = 6 domains.
  • Follow-up emails go out on top of the first emails, so plan for the follow-ups too, or spread the list over more weeks.
Your numbers will be different: These are real results from one campaign. Use them to plan, then switch to your own numbers after 2 to 4 weeks of sending.

Copy-paste: the hand-off brief prompt

Paste this into ChatGPT or Claude with your client details. It turns your notes into the one-page brief that whoever runs your outbound needs.

Hand-off brief prompt
I run a [type of business]. I want to hand off my cold email outbound (list building, inbox setup, sending and reply handling) to someone else, and only do the sales calls myself.

Here are my 3 best clients and why: [paste]
Here are clients I don't want: [paste]
What I sell, in one sentence: [paste]
One real result with a number: [paste]
My booking link and the days I take sales calls: [paste]

Write a one-page hand-off brief with these sections:
1. Ideal client: industry, company size, location, job title, and a trigger (what is happening in their business)
2. Do not contact: who to leave out
3. Offer and proof, in plain words
4. Reply rules: which replies the outbound person answers, and which ones go to me
5. Weekly scorecard: the 5 numbers I want every Monday

Use short, simple sentences. Don't invent results or numbers. If something is missing, list it as a question at the end.
Reply rules message (send to whoever runs outbound)
Hi [name], here's how I'd like replies handled:

- Interested: answer the same working day with my booking link: [link]. Send me a short note with who they are and what they said.
- Price or process questions: use the answers in our FAQ doc, then offer a call.
- Anything you're unsure about: forward it to me the same day.
- Not interested or not now: thank them and stop.
- Remove me: remove them right away.
- Out of office: no action.

Every Monday, please send: people contacted, bounce rate, reply rate, interested replies, calls booked and calls held.

Thanks!

Quick answers

Will I lose control of what goes out under my name?

Not if you approve the offer, proof and first sequence once, keep the domains and inboxes in your name, and check the Monday scorecard. Changes get tested small first.

Do I still need to be on the sales calls?

Yes. Closing stays with you. Buyers of a service want to meet the person who will do the work. You hand off everything before the call.

How long until the first calls?

Plan for about 2 weeks of warm-up before campaigns start. The list gets built during that time, so sending can begin as soon as warm-up is done.

What if I only have a few hours a week for sales?

That's the point of the checklist. After the one-time setup (a few hours), your weekly part is the 15-minute scorecard plus the calls themselves.
