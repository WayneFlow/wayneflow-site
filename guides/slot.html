The short answer

Your number = emails sent ÷ deals closed. It's how many cold emails it takes you to win one deal. In the campaign in the reel, one client sent 84,643 emails in one real month and closed 37 deals. 84,643 ÷ 37 = 2,287.6, so their number was about 2,288 emails per deal.

Think of it like a machine. Every 2,288 emails that go in, one deal comes out. Send fewer than your number in a month, and zero deals is the expected result, not bad luck.

Why one number beats five percentages

  • It connects effort to money. Reply rates and open rates don't pay you. Deals do. Emails per deal tells you how much sending one deal really takes.
  • It makes planning simple. Want 5 deals a month? You need about 5 × your number in emails. No guessing.
  • It tells you what each email is worth. Your deal value ÷ your number = the value of one email sent. That shows you what you can afford to spend.
  • It explains a bad month. If you sent 3,000 emails and your number is 2,288, you should expect about 1 deal. Zero isn't a mystery, it's a volume problem.

Step 1: Find your number from your own results

Why: your number depends on your offer, your price and your buyers. Another company's number can be 10 times different from yours. How:

  1. Pick a time window with steady sending, at least one full month.
  2. From your sending tool, write down total emails sent in that window (every step, follow-ups included).
  3. From your CRM or notes, count the deals closed that came from those emails.
  4. Divide: emails ÷ deals = your number.
  5. Also write down replies, so you can see replies per deal (replies ÷ deals) for the problem finder below.
Count every email: Use total emails sent, not people contacted. Follow-ups cost the same inbox capacity as first emails, so they belong in the number.

Step 2: No deals yet? Estimate it

Why: you need a first plan before you have data. How: multiply your funnel rates, then divide 1 by the result.

Emails per deal = 1 ÷ (reply rate × interested rate × booked rate × close rate)

3% × 24% × 22% × 20% = 0.0003168. Then 1 ÷ 0.0003168 = about 3,157 emails per deal. Use it for your first plan, then switch to your real number once you've closed a few deals. One deal is too few to trust; 3 to 5 gives you a much steadier number. For the full stage-by-stage version, see our funnel math guide.

At 200 emails a week, the same machine pays out about once every 11 weeks (2,288 ÷ 200 = 11.4). At 12,000 emails a month, the same rate gives about 5 deals a month (12,000 ÷ 2,288 = 5.2). Same emails, same offer. Only the volume changed.

Step 4: Work out what one email is worth

Why: this tells you if cold email makes sense for your price, and how much you can spend on lists, inboxes and tools. How: deal value ÷ your number.

  1. Take your average deal value. Example: $2,500 (the average deal across our clients).
  2. Divide by your number: $2,500 ÷ 2,288 = about $1.09 per email sent.
  3. Compare that with what one email costs you (list, verification, inboxes, sending tool, your time). If an email is worth more than it costs, more volume makes more money.
  4. If your deal is small, your number has to be small too. Fix the funnel (Step 6) before you add volume.

Step 5: Plan your monthly volume

Why: now you can turn a deal goal into a sending goal. How: deals wanted × your number = emails a month.

Worked example

  1. Goal: 4 deals a month. Your number: 2,288.
  2. Emails a month: 4 × 2,288 = 9,152.
  3. Emails a week: 9,152 ÷ 4.33 = about 2,114.
  4. Emails a working day: 2,114 ÷ 5 = about 423.
  5. Inboxes at 30 cold emails each per day: 423 ÷ 30 = 14.1, so 15 inboxes. Our MATH guide covers the setup and warm-up.

Step 6: Lower your number (problem finder)

Why: a lower number means more deals from the same sending. How: split your number into emails per reply and replies per deal, and see which half is weak.

Common mistakes

  • Counting people instead of emails. Your number should include follow-ups, or your volume plan will come up short.
  • Trusting a number from one deal. Wait for a few deals before you plan big volume on it.
  • Mixing campaigns. An offer to dentists and an offer to software companies have different numbers. Work them out separately.
  • Blaming the email after a low-volume month. If you sent less than your number, zero deals was the math, not the copy.
  • Pushing volume without warm inboxes. More volume only helps if the emails land in the inbox.

Quick answers

Is 2,288 a good number to aim for?

It's one real campaign's number, not a target. Your number depends on your offer, price and buyers. Our construction software campaign needed about 27,000 emails per annual contract and still paid off, because each contract was a full year of revenue.

The reel says 1 in 28 people replied. Why does this say 1 reply per 35 emails?

The reel counts people. This sheet counts emails, and each person can get several emails in a sequence. Both are right; they measure different things.

How long until I know my real number?

Use the estimate from Step 2 for your first month. Once you've closed a few deals from steady sending, switch to your real number and update it every month.

Should I just send more if my number is high?

Only if one email is worth more than it costs you (Step 4) and your inboxes are healthy. Otherwise, lower the number first with the problem finder.
