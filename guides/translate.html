The Come-Back Script: What to Say When a Prospect Says "Not Now"

"Not now" is not "no"

Busy people rarely say a hard no. They say "not now", "maybe next quarter" or "send me something". Most sellers read this as a polite rejection and move on.

Often it means exactly what it says: the timing is wrong, not the offer. Their budget resets later. Another project comes first. Their contract with someone else ends in six months. If you give up, you lose a buyer who would have said yes later.

The corporate translation table

Here's what common "not now" replies usually mean, and what to do with each. These are patterns, not rules. When you're not sure, ask.

The 3-step come-back script

Step 1: Ask for the date

Makes sense. When's a better time to come back to this?

Why it works: you agree with them first, so there's no pressure. Then you ask a question that's easy to answer. They choose the date, so when you come back, you're not chasing them. You're doing what they asked.

If you want a softer or more specific version, use one of these:

  • "Totally fair. Should I check back in 3 months, or is there a better time?"
  • "No problem. Is there a date when this gets looked at again, like budget planning?"
  • "Got it. Would early [month] be a good time to pick this up?"
Vague answer?: If they say "sometime next year", turn it into a real date for them: "Would mid-January work?" A vague answer is easy to forget. A date is not.

Step 2: Write their date in your CRM, with a reminder

Why it matters: no date, and you'll both forget. Three months later, they won't remember the email, and you won't remember who said what. The note you write today is what makes the come-back message work.

  1. Log it within 5 minutes of their reply, while it's fresh.
  2. Copy their exact words about timing and the reason. You'll use them later.
  3. Set the reminder for 2 to 3 days before their date, so you have time to find something new.
  4. Tag the lead "come-back" so you can see all of them in one list.
  5. Once a week, look at the list and check nothing slipped past its date.

Step 3: Come back on their date with their words + something new

You said to come back after this date. Since then, a similar company got this result. Worth picking up?

Why it works: their own words remind them that they asked you to come back. That makes it feel like a promise you kept, not a cold email. The new thing gives them a reason to reply now. "Just checking in" gives them nothing.

  • A real result from a similar company (only if it's true).
  • Something that changed on your side: a new service, a lower starting price, a pilot option.
  • Something that changed on their side: a new hire, a new office, funding, a new product.
  • One useful idea for the problem they mentioned, even if they never buy.
Come-back email template
Subject: [their words, e.g. after Q1 planning]

Hi [first name],

Back in [month] you said to come back to this [their words, e.g. after Q1 planning]. So here I am.

Since then, [something new: a similar company got this result / we added this / I saw you did this].

Worth picking up?

[your name]

Worked example

  1. October 14: Dana replies: "Interesting, but let's circle back next quarter."
  2. October 14: You reply: "Makes sense. When's a better time to come back to this?" Dana says: "After our Q1 planning, mid-March."
  3. October 14: You log it: come-back date March 18, reminder March 15, her words "after our Q1 planning".
  4. March 15: The reminder fires. You find something new: a case study from another logistics company.
  5. March 18: You send: "Hi Dana, back in October you said to come back after Q1 planning. Since then, we ran a campaign for another logistics firm and they got [real result]. Worth picking up?"

Now the example math. Say 40 people tell you "not now" this quarter. If you give up, that's 40 lost conversations. If you get dates from 30 of them and even 5 are ready when you come back, that's 5 new conversations from leads you already paid to get.

If they say "not now" again

It happens. Ask for a new date once more and log it again. If it's the third "not now", ask a direct, friendly question: "Should I close the file on this for now?" A clear yes or no is better than a lead that floats forever.

There's data behind this too. In the Gong x 30 Minutes to President's Club cold email report (85M+ cold emails), follow-up emails with breakup language nearly doubled the reply rate. People reply when you make it easy to say where things stand.

Quick answers

Isn't coming back after a "not now" annoying?

Not if they gave you the date. You're doing what they asked. It only feels annoying when you come back early or with nothing new.

What if they won't give me a date?

Suggest one: "Should I check back in 3 months?" Most people say yes. Log it like any other date.

How long should I keep coming back?

Ask for a new date up to two times. On the third "not now", ask if you should close the file. Respect a clear no right away.

What if I don't have a new result to share?

Share one useful idea for the problem they mentioned, or something that changed on their side. Never invent a result.
