What the data says

Smartlead looked at more than 850 million emails sent through its platform. It grouped each email by the hour it was sent, in the recipient's local time. Replies peaked at 7am, with a 1.34% reply rate. That's about 1 reply for every 75 people you email.

The afternoon was much weaker. From 1pm to 4pm, reply rates sat between 0.82% and 0.86%, or about 1 reply per 116 to 122 people.

The day of the week mattered much less. On the same page, Smartlead says reply rates across the week sat between 0.82% and 0.91%. So stop worrying about Tuesday vs Thursday. Worry about the hour.

Why 7am works

  • You land on top. At 7am most inboxes are quiet. Your email is one of the first things they see, not number 40 under a pile of afternoon messages.
  • People read email before the day starts. Many people check their phone before their first meeting. A short email is easy to answer then.
  • The afternoon is busy. By 2pm people are in meetings and clearing their inbox fast. A cold email is easy to skip.
Be honest about the data: This is one platform's data, and it shows when replies happened, not why. Use 7am as your starting point, then test it on your own list.

Worked example: same list, more replies

Say you email 1,000 people with the same email. Using the Smartlead averages:

  1. At 7am their time: 1,000 × 1.34% = about 13 replies.
  2. At 1pm to 4pm their time: 1,000 × 0.82% to 0.86% = about 8 to 9 replies.
  3. Same email, same people: about 4 to 5 extra replies just from timing.
  4. At 5,000 people a month: about 67 replies at 7am vs 41 to 43 in the afternoon.

Your own numbers will be different. The point is the gap, not the exact count.

The big catch: use THEIR time zone

7am only works if it's 7am for the reader. If you're in New York and send at 7am your time, a lead in Los Angeles gets it at 4am, and a lead in London gets it at noon. By the time they look, it's buried.

So don't schedule by your clock. Schedule a 7am to 9am window in each lead's local time. Most of your emails then land between 7 and 9, close to the peak.

Daylight saving: Clocks move during the year, and not on the same dates everywhere. In the US they move forward on the second Sunday of March. The UK and Europe change on the last Sunday of March and October. Sydney moves the opposite way. The easy fix: in your tool, pick a city time zone (like America/Chicago), not a fixed offset like UTC-6. Then the tool handles the change for you.

How to set it up, step by step

  1. Add a time-zone column to your list. If your lead data has city, state or country, you can work out the time zone from that. Many lead databases already include it.
  2. Fill the gaps. No location? Use the company's head office. Still nothing? Put those leads in a separate group and send them in your main market's 7 to 9am.
  3. Check your sending tool. If it can send by each lead's own time zone, turn that on and set the window to 7:00 to 9:00am.
  4. If it can't, split the list. Make one campaign (or one schedule) per time zone, using the table above. Same email, different window.
  5. Space the sends out. Set a gap between emails from each inbox so they don't all go at 7:00 on the dot. A steady flow looks more natural.
  6. Test for 2 to 4 weeks. Run half your list at 7 to 9am and half at your old time. Compare replies per 1,000 people, not opens.

Worked example: does a 2-hour window fit?

Say you send from 10 inboxes, and each one sends 30 cold emails a day. That's 300 emails a day.

  1. A 7 to 9am window is 120 minutes.
  2. Each inbox needs to send 30 emails in 120 minutes: one email about every 4 minutes.
  3. That's a normal, slow pace. If you need more volume, add inboxes. Don't widen the window to all day.

Quick answers

Is 7am right for every industry?

Not always. Smartlead's data is an average across many senders and doesn't break it down by industry. Start at 7am, then test it against your old time on your own list.

What about follow-up emails?

Use the same 7 to 9am window for follow-ups. The timing logic is the same for every email in the sequence.

Should I send on weekends?

The data says the day matters much less than the hour. Most B2B senders stick to weekdays. If you test weekends, measure replies, not opens.

My leads are all in one city. Do I still need this?

No time-zone split needed. Just set your window to 7 to 9am in their city's time zone, which may not be yours.
