The number from the reel

One re-engagement campaign sent to 1,532 ghosted prospects got an 11.75% reply rate. That comes from cold email operator Christian Plascencia's post, summed up by Cold Email Daily. 11.75% of 1,532 is about 180 replies from people most senders had written off.

For context, Hunter's State of Email Outreach 2026 puts the average cold email reply rate at 4.5%, and only 3% for sales emails. So a good re-engagement campaign can beat a brand-new one. The reason is simple: these people already know your name, they fit your target, and most of them didn't say no. They just didn't answer.

Why "dead" leads usually aren't dead

  • Timing was wrong. They were busy, mid-project or had no budget that month. Months later, that can be different.
  • Your email got buried. A no-reply is not a no. Most people never decided anything about you.
  • Their situation changed. New boss, new funding, new hires, a new problem. People and companies change all the time.
  • Your offer changed. You may have a new result, a new service or a better price than when you first wrote.

Contact data also goes old fast. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports that the median worker had been with their current employer 4.1 years (January 2026). That's the middle of the range, so plenty of people move much sooner. Christian's advice in the same post is to re-check anything older than six months before you email it again.

Step 1: Pick who to re-engage (and who never to touch)

Why: the fastest way to hurt your domain is to email people who already asked you to stop. The fastest way to get replies is to email people who were a good fit but got the timing wrong.

  1. Export every lead from old campaigns with their status: no reply, replied, bounced, unsubscribed.
  2. Remove everyone who asked to stop, unsubscribed or marked you as spam. Add them to your sending tool's block list.
  3. Remove anyone who is already in a live campaign, so nobody gets two emails at once.
  4. Split the rest into the groups in the table above. Each group gets its own campaign.

Step 2: Clean the list again

Why: old lists bounce. Bounces tell inbox providers you're emailing strangers, and that pushes all your emails toward spam. Hunter's average bounce rate is 3.6%. Keep yours under 2%.

  1. Run the whole list through an email verifier again, even if you verified it the first time.
  2. Remove invalid addresses. Send "risky" and catch-all addresses later, in a small separate batch.
  3. Check that the person still has the same job. A quick look at their LinkedIn profile or the company's team page is enough for your best leads.
  4. If they left, find who took their role. That new person is a fresh lead, and you have a good reason to write: "I used to talk with Sarah about this."

Step 3: Find one new reason to write

Why: "just checking in" gives nobody a reason to reply. A re-engagement email works when something is different from last time. That one new thing is the whole email.

A new offer or lower risk"We now run a small 2-week pilot before any long contract."

Step 4: The 3-email re-engagement script

We follow the same rules on client campaigns: verify again, give a new reason, keep it short, and stop the moment someone says no. Start a new thread with a new subject line. The old thread is months old and may already be buried. Keep each email under 80 words, plain text, and no links in the first email.

Email 1 (day 1): the new reason
Subject: {{first_name}}, quick update

Hi {{first_name}},

I wrote to you back in {{last_month}} about {{problem_you_solve}}. Probably bad timing.

One thing changed since then: {{what_changed}}.

Is {{problem_you_solve}} still on your list at {{company}}, or should I close the file?

{{your_name}}
Email 2 (day 4): give something useful
Subject: Re: {{first_name}}, quick update

Hi {{first_name}},

No need to reply to the last one. I put together {{free_resource}} for {{their_role}}s at companies like {{company}}. It shows {{one_specific_thing}}.

Want me to send it over?

{{your_name}}
Email 3 (day 9): the polite close
Subject: Re: {{first_name}}, quick update

Hi {{first_name}},

I'll stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox.

If {{problem_you_solve}} comes up later, just reply to this email and I'll pick it up.

All the best,
{{your_name}}
Pro tip: A polite close often gets replies. People answer "yes, still interested" or "no thanks" because it's easy. Both answers are useful.

Step 5: Send it slowly and watch two numbers

Why: re-engaged lists carry more spam-complaint risk than fresh lists, because some people remember you and didn't want the first email either. Google's sender guidelines ask senders to keep spam reports under 0.3%, and to aim for under 0.1%.

  1. Send re-engagement emails from warmed-up inboxes that are already healthy, never from brand-new ones.
  2. Start with your best group ("not now" replies), then add the no-reply group.
  3. Watch bounce rate (under 2%) and spam complaints (as close to zero as you can) every day for the first week.
  4. If someone replies "no", stop and remove them from every campaign right away.
  5. After the 3 emails, leave the rest alone. Don't put them back into a new re-engagement round for at least 6 months.

Worked example

Say you have 1,500 old leads who never replied. You re-verify the list and remove 200 bad addresses, so 1,300 are left. You send the 3-email script.

Then pick the one you would most likely reply to and say why in one line.

Quick answers

How long should I wait before re-engaging?

At least 60 to 90 days after your last email. For people who said "not interested", wait 6 months or more and only write if something real changed.

Should I reply in the old thread or start a new one?

Start a new thread with a new subject line. The old one is months old and buried. Mention the old email in one short line so it doesn't feel like a trick.

Can I re-engage people who unsubscribed?

No. Anyone who asked to stop stays on your do-not-email list forever.

What if the person left the company?

Find who took their role and email them as a new lead. Mentioning the person they replaced is a natural opener.
