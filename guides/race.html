The math from the reel

Sending by hand, most people manage about 200 cold emails a week, and only in the weeks when client work leaves time. A system sends every day whether you're busy or not.

This is plain math, not a promise of results. More emails out means more people who can say yes, but only if the emails land in the inbox. The rest of this guide is the setup that makes that happen.

Weekdays only?: Many people send Monday to Friday only. The same 2,800 a week then works out to 2,800 ÷ 5 = 560 a day. The weekly total is the same, you just need a few more inboxes.

Rule 1: Spread the volume over many inboxes

Why: one inbox pushing hundreds of emails to strangers looks like a spammer. Google's limits page allows a paid Google Workspace inbox to send up to 2,000 emails a day, but that's a system limit, not a safe cold email limit. Hunter's 2026 report, based on 31 million emails, found inboxes sending 20 to 49 emails a day got a 5.7% reply rate, above the 4.5% average.

So we use about 30 cold emails a day per inbox and no more than 3 inboxes per domain. Need more volume? Add inboxes. Never push one inbox harder.

Rule 2: Never use your main domain

Why: if a sending domain gets flagged, you want it to be a spare, not the domain your clients and invoices come from. Hunter also found custom domains got 5.2% replies vs 2.5% for free addresses like Gmail, so don't send from free accounts either.

  1. Buy domains that look like your brand: for acme.com, try getacme.com, acmehq.com or tryacme.com.
  2. Pick .com where you can.
  3. Point every sending domain to your real website.
  4. Buy 1 spare domain for every 5 or so, and warm it up too.

Rule 3: Set the records on day 1

Why: at 2,800 emails a week you're sending real volume, so the records matter. Google's sender guidelines require SPF or DKIM for every sender, SPF, DKIM and DMARC for anyone sending 5,000+ messages a day to Gmail, and a spam rate under 0.3% for everyone. Google recommends staying under 0.1%.

  1. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain before warm-up. Our DNS records guide has the exact values.
  2. Set up Google Postmaster Tools for each domain to watch your spam rate.

Rule 5: Feed the system enough leads

Why: a system that sends 2,800 emails a week needs new people every week. If each person gets a 4-email sequence, about a quarter of the weekly volume goes to new contacts.

  1. With a 4-email sequence, 2,800 ÷ 4 = 700 new people a week, or about 2,800 a month.
  2. Verify every address before it goes in. Hunter's average bounce rate was 3.6%. Keep yours under 2%.
  3. Keep each campaign to one type of buyer, so the email fits the reader.
  4. Plan 4 to 7 emails per person. Instantly found 42% of replies come from follow-ups.

Worked example: 400 a day

  1. Target: 2,800 a week, sending 7 days. 2,800 ÷ 7 = 400 a day.
  2. 400 ÷ 30 = 13.3, so round up to 14 inboxes.
  3. 14 ÷ 3 = 4.7, so round up to 5 domains (room for 15 inboxes).
  4. Add 1 spare domain with 3 inboxes, warmed up and ready.
  5. Set SPF, DKIM and DMARC on all 6 domains on day 1.
  6. Weeks 1-2: warm-up only. Week 3: 140 a day. Week 4: 280 a day. Week 5: 420 a day.
  7. Load about 700 new verified contacts each week for a 4-email sequence.

Quick answers

Will 14 times more emails get 14 times more replies?

Not guaranteed. The math only tells you how many people see your offer. Replies depend on the list, the offer and whether emails land in the inbox.

How long until I'm sending 2,800 a week?

About 5 weeks with this plan: 2 weeks of warm-up, then a 3-week ramp. Instantly recommends 4 to 6 weeks for new domains.

Why not just send more from one inbox?

One inbox sending hundreds of cold emails looks like a spammer. Hunter's data shows the best reply rates at 20 to 49 emails a day per inbox.

Should I send on weekends?

You can. If you send weekdays only, the same weekly total needs about 560 a day, so plan for more inboxes.
