Why bursts don't work

Many owners notice around day ten that nothing is booked. Then they do a panic week of outreach, get busy with the new work, and stop. The next month starts empty again. That's a burst pattern, and it always leaves empty days.

Bursts also hurt deliverability. Google's sender guidelines warn against sudden volume spikes and say you can increase volume faster when you send daily instead of weekly. Steady sending is safer, and it keeps calls coming.

Part 1: The list

  1. Pick one buyer: industry, company size and job title.
  2. Build enough leads for a full month, not just a week. (See the math below.)
  3. Verify every address. Instantly says keep bounces under 2%.
  4. Split the list into small groups of one buyer type. Hunter's 2026 report found sequences with 21–50 recipients got 6.2% replies vs 2.4% for 500+.

Part 2: Domains and inboxes

  1. Send from separate lookalike domains, never your main domain.
  2. Set SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain. Our DNS records guide has the values.
  3. Max 3 inboxes per domain, about 30 cold emails per inbox per day at full volume.
  4. Warm up for 14 days, then ramp slowly. Instantly's 2026 report recommends building up over 4 to 6 weeks.
  5. Keep 1 spare domain warming so a tired inbox never stops the flow.

Part 3: The weekly rhythm

Why: a steady flow needs new people entering the sequence every week, and follow-ups going out on schedule. Instantly found 42% of replies come from follow-ups and recommends 4 to 7 touches, 3 to 4 days apart, in a Mon → Wed → Fri rhythm.

Worked example: how many emails for a steady month

Say you send 100 cold emails a day for 30 days and hit Instantly's average reply rate of 3.43%.

  1. 100 × 30 = 3,000 emails a month.
  2. 3,000 × 3.43% = about 103 replies.
  3. In our full-quarter case study, about 1 in 4 replies came from a buyer who wanted to talk (1,061 of 4,372). 103 × 24% = about 25 interested buyers a month.
  4. That's about 6 interested buyers a week, every week, instead of a burst and a gap.
  5. Inboxes needed: 100 ÷ 30 = 3.3, round up to 4 inboxes on 2 domains, plus 1 spare domain.
Use your own numbers: Your reply rate and interest rate will differ. Track them for 4 weeks, then redo this math with your real numbers.

Part 4: Turning replies into calls

  1. Reply fast. A buyer who wrote back today may forget you by next week.
  2. Offer two specific times, plus a booking link.
  3. Send a short reminder the day before the call.
  4. Track every interested reply until it's booked or closed out.

The steady pipeline checklist

  • One buyer type picked and written down
  • A full month of verified leads ready
  • Separate sending domains with SPF, DKIM and DMARC
  • Inboxes warmed for 14 days, then ramped slowly
  • 1 spare domain always warming
  • New leads added every week
  • 4 to 7 touches, Mon → Wed → Fri rhythm
  • Every reply answered within a few hours
  • Weekly check: emails sent, replies, interested, calls booked

Quick answers

Isn't referral business enough?

Referrals are great, but you can't control when they come. A steady outbound system adds calls you can plan around.

How long until calls come in every week?

Plan for about 5 weeks to full volume: 2 weeks of warm-up, then a ramp. Replies start once sending starts.

How many emails do I need?

Work backward from your goal. At a 3.43% reply rate, 3,000 emails a month is about 103 replies. Track your own numbers and adjust.
