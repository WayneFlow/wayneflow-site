The short answer

A slow pipeline has deals in it that move slowly. An empty pipeline has nothing in it, because nothing went in. If 0 goes in the top, 0 comes out the bottom. No sales trick fixes that.

Here's what a full tank looked like in the campaign in the reel: one client, one real month.

StageNumberWhat it means
Emails into the top84,643About 2,800 a day if you spread it over 30 days
Replies2,399About 1 reply for every 35 emails
Deals closed37About 2,288 emails and about 65 replies per deal

Read it backwards. Every one of those 37 deals needed about 2,288 emails to go in first. Stop filling the top and the deals stop too, just a few weeks later. That's the whole idea: fill the top, and the bottom takes care of itself.

Why the top decides everything

There's a delay. What you put in today turns into replies over the next days and weeks. Instantly's 2026 benchmark report found 58% of replies come from the first email and 42% from follow-ups, and it suggests spacing follow-ups 3 to 4 days apart. So a 3-email sequence takes about a week to finish for each person. If you skip a week of filling the top, you don't feel it that week. You feel it later, when the calendar is suddenly empty.

Steady beats bursts. Instantly found teams that keep sending consistently see 15 to 20% more replies, and warns that sending 500 on Monday, nothing for three days, then 1,000 on Friday looks suspicious to email providers. Google's sender guidelines say the same thing: send at a consistent rate and avoid bursts.

The setup checklist

Three steps, the same three from the reel. Each has the why, then exactly how.

Step 1: A list of people who fit

Why: volume only helps if it reaches buyers. Hunter's State of Email Outreach 2026 found 61% of decision makers say cold emails fail because they aren't relevant. A full tank of the wrong people is still empty where it counts.

  • Write your fit rules: industry, company size, location, buyer job title, and one sign they need you now.
  • Contact 1 or 2 people per company. Hunter found that got a 5.1% reply rate vs 3.5% for 3 or more.
  • Verify every email before it goes in. Instantly says to keep bounces under 2%.
  • Build next month's list before this one runs out (our lead fuel calculator gives you the date).
  • Split the list into small segments with their own first line. Hunter found groups of 21 to 50 people got 6.2% replies vs 2.4% for 500+.

Step 2: A setup that lands in the inbox

Why: an email in spam is an email that never went in. Gmail and Outlook both have hard rules now. Google requires anyone sending 5,000+ emails a day to Gmail to use SPF, DKIM and DMARC and to keep the spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%. Since May 5, 2025, Microsoft requires the same three records for domains sending over 5,000 emails a day to Outlook.com, and mail that fails can be rejected.

  • Send from separate domains that look like your brand, never your main domain. Hunter found a custom domain got 5.2% replies vs 2.5% for free Gmail-style addresses.
  • Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC to every sending domain, even if you're far below 5,000 a day.
  • Warm up new inboxes. Instantly suggests starting at 5 to 10 emails a day and building up over 4 to 6 weeks.
  • Keep each inbox to about 30 cold emails a day. Hunter found 20 to 49 a day per inbox got a 5.7% reply rate, above the 4.5% average.
  • Connect Google Postmaster Tools and keep the spam rate under 0.1%, as Google recommends.

Step 3: Volume, every day

Why: a tank fills a little every day, not once a month. Steady daily sending gets more replies (Instantly) and builds trust with email providers (Google). It also means replies, calls and deals arrive steadily instead of in feast-or-famine waves.

Work out your daily number

  1. Deals you want per month.
  2. Emails per deal. Use your own if you have it. If not, use a careful guess and replace it after your first full month (our funnel math sheet shows how to build one).
  3. Emails per month = deals × emails per deal.
  4. Emails per day = emails per month ÷ 22 working days.
  5. New people per day = emails per day ÷ emails in your sequence.
  6. Inboxes = emails per day ÷ 30. Our sending calculator covers the domains and warm-up.

Worked example

Say you want 5 deals a month and you borrow the ratio from the campaign in the reel, about 2,288 emails per deal. Your sequence has 3 emails.

StepMathResult
Emails per month5 × 2,28811,440

Inboxes at 30 a day520 ÷ 30 = 17.3, round up18 inboxes

Now see what one missed week costs. Five working days without new people is about 870 people (174 × 5) who never enter, and about 2,610 emails (870 × 3) never sent. At about 2,288 emails per deal, that's about 1 deal that never happens. You'd notice it weeks later, not that week.

Your ratio will be different: 2,288 emails per deal came from one client in one month. In our construction software case, 162,073 emails led to 6 annual contracts, about 27,000 emails per contract. Bigger, slower deals need a much bigger tank. Use your own number as soon as you have it.

Common mistakes

  • Calling an empty pipeline "slow". If nothing went in this month, waiting won't help.
  • Filling the top only when sales are down. By then you're weeks behind.
  • More volume from the same few inboxes. Push past about 30 a day each and you trade the tank for the spam folder.
  • Counting emails, not people. Follow-ups don't fill the tank. New people do.
  • No tracker. If you don't write down what goes in, you won't see the gap until the calendar is empty.

Quick answers

How long until a full tank turns into calls?

If your inboxes are already warmed up, replies start in the first days and keep coming as follow-ups go out. If you're starting from zero, add 4 to 6 weeks of warm-up first, as Instantly suggests. That's why you fill the top before you need the calls.

Do I really need to send every single day?

Every working day is what matters for most B2B sellers. Hunter found the day of the week didn't move reply rates in its data. What hurts is stopping and starting, so pick a daily number you can keep.

I don't know my emails per deal. What do I use?

Start with a careful guess from our funnel math sheet, send for a full month, then replace the guess with your real number. Don't copy the reel's 2,288 blindly; it came from one client.

Is more volume always better?

No. More right-fit people is better. More of the wrong people, or more emails from the same few inboxes, only adds bounces and spam risk. Grow the tank by adding verified people and warmed inboxes together.
