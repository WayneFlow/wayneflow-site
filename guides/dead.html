First, what "normal" looks like

Before fixing anything, know the target. Instantly's 2026 benchmark report puts the average reply rate at 3.43%. The top quarter of senders get 5.5% or more, and the top 10% get 10.7% or more. Hunter's 2026 report found a 4.5% average, but only 3% for sales emails. If you're under 1%, something below is broken.

Fix 1: Turn open tracking off

Why: open tracking adds a tiny hidden image to every email. Spam filters notice it, and it turns a plain-text email into an HTML one. In Hunter's data, campaigns with tracking off got 7.4% replies vs 4.4% with it on.

Open rates aren't even reliable anymore. Apple Mail and some security tools open emails automatically, so your "open rate" includes robots.

  1. In your sending tool, turn off open tracking for every campaign.
  2. Turn off link tracking too, or set up a custom tracking domain if you really need clicks.
  3. Track what pays you instead: replies, interested replies and booked calls.

Fix 2: Send to smaller, tighter lists

Why: a small list forces you to write for one kind of buyer. In Hunter's data, campaigns sent to 21 to 50 people got 6.2% replies vs 2.4% for 500+ people. The average campaign went to 449 people, so most senders are on the wrong side of this.

Hunter also found that emailing 1 or 2 people per company got 5.1% replies vs 3.5% for 3 or more. Emailing the whole team at once looks like spam to them and to filters.

  1. Split your big list by industry, job title, company size and location.
  2. Inside each group, look for a shared trigger: hiring, new funding, a new location.
  3. Write one version of the email for each small group, with a first line about what they have in common.
  4. Contact 1 or 2 people per company, not 5.

Fix 3: Verify every email before you send

Why: bounces tell inbox providers you're emailing people you don't know. Hunter's average bounce rate was 3.6%. Keep yours under 2%. Above that, your other emails start landing in spam.

  1. Run every list through an email verifier before it goes into a campaign, even a list you just bought.
  2. Remove invalid addresses. Remove "risky" ones, or send them later from a separate small campaign.
  3. Be careful with "catch-all" addresses (domains that accept everything). Send to them only in small batches and watch bounces.
  4. Verify again if a list is more than a month or two old. People change jobs all the time.

Our own campaigns run between 0.45% and 3.08% bounce, and we pause any inbox that goes over the line until the list is cleaned.

Why: busy people read email on their phone and decide in seconds. Instantly's 2026 report found the best campaigns keep emails under 80 words.

  • Under 80 words, so it fits on one phone screen without scrolling
  • Plain text: no images, no logos, no fancy design
  • No link in the first email, or one at most
  • One idea per email
  • End with one small question, like "Worth a look?", not "Can we book 30 minutes?"

Fix 5: Send smart follow-ups (and know when to stop)

Why: Instantly found that 58% of replies come from the first email and 42% come from follow-ups. Stop after one email and you lose almost half your replies. The same report found the best sequences are 4 to 7 emails, spaced 3 to 7 days apart.

EmailDayWhat to say
1Day 1The main email: about them, one result, small question.
2Day 4A new angle: a different problem you solve, or a short case study.
3Day 8Make it easy: offer something useful, like a short teardown or a free resource.
4Day 15The polite close: "Should I stop reaching out about this?" These often get replies.
  • Never send "just bumping this up". Every follow-up needs a new reason to reply.
  • Reply in the same thread, so they can see the earlier email.
  • Stop immediately when someone replies, even with a "no".
  • Don't go past 7 emails. Each extra email adds more spam-report risk.

Your weekly checklist

  1. Open tracking and link tracking are off.
  2. Every list sent this week was verified first.
  3. Bounce rate is under 2% on every inbox.
  4. Each campaign goes to a small group with one shared reason to care.
  5. Every email is under 80 words and plain text.
  6. Every follow-up has a new angle.
  7. Interested replies get an answer the same day.
Bottom line: Cold email isn't dead. Bad cold email is.

Quick answers

If tracking is off, how do I know it's working?

Count replies, interested replies and booked calls. Those are the numbers that pay you anyway.

What's a good reply rate?

Around 3 to 4.5% is average, 5.5% or more is top-quarter and 10% or more is excellent, based on Instantly's and Hunter's 2026 reports. Track interested replies too, since a reply can also be a no.

How many follow-ups is too many?

Instantly's data points to 4 to 7 emails in total. We usually stop at 4 or 5, because spam-report risk grows with each extra email.

Is cold email legal?

In the U.S., yes, if you follow CAN-SPAM rules. Our legal guide explains it in plain English. Other countries have their own rules.
