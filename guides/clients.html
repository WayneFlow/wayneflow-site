The short answer

Start from the money and work backwards, one step at a time: goal ÷ price = clients, clients × close rate = calls, calls × emails per call = emails, emails ÷ working days ÷ 30 = inboxes. Four divisions and you know exactly how much outreach your goal needs.

StepMathWorked example (all example numbers)
1. ClientsMonthly goal ÷ price per client$10,000 ÷ $2,500 = 4 clients
2. CallsClients × calls per closeClose 1 in 4 calls: 4 × 4 = 16 calls
3. EmailsCalls × emails per call booked1 call per 190 emails: 16 × 190 = 3,040 emails (about 3,000)
4. Emails a dayEmails ÷ working days3,040 ÷ 22 = 138.2, round up to 139 a day (about 140)
5. InboxesEmails a day ÷ 30 per inbox139 ÷ 30 = 4.6, round up to 5 inboxes
Important: Every number in the example is just an example: the $2,500 price, the 1 in 4 close rate and the 1 call per 190 emails. The 30 emails per inbox per day is a common rule. Swap in your own numbers, or the answer means nothing.

Why work backwards

Most people pick a sending number first ("let's send 100 a day") and hope the money follows. Then they don't know if 100 is too much, too little or way off. Working backwards flips it. You start with the one number you actually care about, money, and every other number falls out of it.

It also turns a big scary goal into a small daily habit. "$10K a month" is hard to act on. "Send 139 emails today from 5 inboxes" is a to-do item.

Where to get your real numbers

  • Price (B): what one new client pays you in the month you sign them. If you sell a monthly retainer, use one month's fee, and remember the goal here is new revenue each month.
  • Close rate (C): look at your last 10 to 20 sales calls. If you closed 3 of 12, that's 1 in 4. No history yet? Use 1 in 5 to stay safe, then update it after your first calls.
  • Emails per call (D): total cold emails sent (all follow-ups count) ÷ calls booked, from your last campaign. If you've never sent cold email, use the example to plan, then replace it with your real number after 2 to 3 weeks of sending.
  • Working days (E): usually 20 to 22. Take out holidays.
  • Emails per inbox (F): 30 a day is a common safe rule. In Hunter's 2026 report (31 million emails), inboxes sending 20 to 49 a day got a 5.7% reply rate, above the 4.5% average.

Emails per call changes everything

The number that moves your plan the most is emails per call booked. It depends on your list, your offer and your market, and it can be very different from the 190 in the example. Here's the same $10K goal (4 clients, 16 calls) with three different emails-per-call numbers:

Common mistakes

  • Using someone else's close rate or emails-per-call number instead of your own.
  • Forgetting that calls come a few weeks after the emails go out. Start this month for next month's revenue.
  • Counting only first emails, not follow-ups, when you work out emails per call.
  • Pushing one inbox to 150 a day instead of adding inboxes.
  • Never updating the sheet. Re-run it every month with last month's real numbers.

Quick answers

I've never sent cold email. What emails-per-call number do I use?

Plan with the example, but treat it as a guess. After 2 to 3 weeks of sending, divide emails sent by calls booked and put your real number in the sheet.

I sell a monthly retainer. Does this still work?

Yes. Use one month's fee as the price. The calculator tells you how many new clients you need each month; your total monthly revenue grows as clients stay.

Why 30 emails per inbox?

It's a common safe rule that keeps inboxes out of spam. Hunter's data showed the best reply rates in the 20 to 49 a day range. Need more volume? Add inboxes, don't push one harder.

Why is my answer so different from the reel?

Because the reel uses example numbers. Your price, close rate and emails per call are different, so your plan will be too. That's the point of the calculator.
