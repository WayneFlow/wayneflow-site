The short answer

When someone replies "interested", don't send a calendar link. Reply fast, from one person, with one specific time: "Does Thursday at 10:00 am ET work?" A yes takes them two seconds. Then send the person who runs the call a short brief, so the call starts where the email left off. One time. One yes.

Why a calendar link stalls the deal

A calendar link feels helpful. To the prospect, it's homework. They have to click, open a new page, check their own calendar, compare dozens of slots, pick one and fill in a form. Every step is a place to stop. Many people mean to do it later, and later never comes. That's the 7 days of silence from the reel.

There is research on what too many options do. In the famous jam study by Sheena Iyengar and Mark Lepper, described by Stanford Magazine, a booth with 24 jams got more shoppers to stop (60% vs 40%), but only 3% of the people at the 24-jam booth bought, vs 30% at the 6-jam booth.

Be honest about the science: Choice overload doesn't show up every time. A 2010 review of 50 experiments found the average effect was close to zero. So the main reason to drop the link isn't the jam study. It's the work: a link asks for 6 steps, a time asks for one word.

Template 1: the one-time reply

Use this for any clear "interested", "tell me more" or "sure, let's talk". Keep it under 50 words. Pick a real time in their time zone, 1 to 2 working days out, so it's soon but not rushed.

Copy-paste: interested reply
Hi [first name],

Great, glad it's useful.

Does [day] at [time] [their time zone] work for a 15-minute call? I'll send the invite as soon as you say yes.

[your name]
Copy-paste: they asked a question first
Hi [first name],

Good question. [One or two sentence answer.]

Easier to show you than to type it out. Does [day] at [time] [their time zone] work for 15 minutes?

[your name]
Send the invite right away: The moment they say yes, send the calendar invite with the call link inside. Then reply "Invite sent, talk [day]." Don't make them wait for it.

Template 2: when they say no to your time

A "no, I can't do Thursday" is still a yes to the call. Don't send the link now either. Give two new times, or ask for theirs. Two options is still easy to answer.

Copy-paste: they can't make it
No problem. Would [day] at [time] or [day] at [time] [their time zone] work better?

If neither fits, send me a time that does and I'll make it work.
Copy-paste: they ask for a link
Of course, here it is: [booking link]

If it's easier, just reply with a time and I'll send the invite myself.
Copy-paste: no answer after 2 working days
Hi [first name], still keen to show you [the one result from your email].

Would [day] at [time] [their time zone] work instead?

The one-owner rule

Interested replies get lost when they land in a shared inbox where everyone thinks someone else will answer. Fix it with one rule: every interested reply goes to one named person. That person answers it, books the time and writes the brief. Nobody else touches it.

  1. Pick one owner for each block of working hours, plus one named backup for when they're in a meeting or off.
  2. Send every reply from every sending inbox into one view. In Instantly, that's the Unibox. Mark each positive reply as Interested so the owner can filter to just those (Instantly's guide).
  3. Turn on phone alerts for new replies for the owner only. An alert sent to the whole team is an alert nobody owns.
  4. The owner answers from the same thread the prospect replied to. Never start a new email from a different person.
  5. At the end of each day, the owner checks: is every Interested reply either booked or waiting on a new time?

Speed targets

The best-known data on speed comes from the InsideSales.com/MIT Lead Response Management Study (original PDF), led by Dr. James Oldroyd in 2007. It looked at 3 years of data from 6 companies: over 15,000 web leads and over 100,000 call attempts. It measured phone calls to people who had filled in a web form, not replies to cold email. Still, the pattern is clear:

  • The odds of reaching a lead were 100 times lower when the first call came at 30 minutes instead of 5.
  • The odds of qualifying a lead were 21 times lower at 30 minutes vs 5 minutes.
  • Even between 5 and 10 minutes, the odds of reaching the lead dropped about 5 times.

Newer data says the same. In its 2021 lead response study of 5.7 million inbound leads at 400+ companies, InsideSales found conversion rates were more than 8x higher when the first attempt came in the first 5 minutes vs between 5 minutes and 24 hours. Yet only 0.1% of leads were contacted that fast.

Worked example: what slow replies cost

Here's how to work out your own loss. The inputs below are example numbers, not benchmarks. Swap in your own from the last 30 days.

  1. You get 60 interested replies a month. Today, 20 are answered within 5 minutes and 40 are answered later the same day or the next day.
  2. Your fast replies book a call 40% of the time (example input).
  3. InsideSales found the first 5 minutes convert more than 8x better. Use 8 to stay on the safe side: slow replies book 40% ÷ 8 = 5%.
  4. Meetings today: 20 × 40% = 8, plus 40 × 5% = 2. Total: 10 meetings.
  5. If all 60 were answered within 5 minutes: 60 × 40% = 24 meetings.
  6. Meetings lost to slow replies: 24 − 10 = 14 a month, or 14 × 12 = 168 a year.
  7. Turn that into money with your own numbers. Example: if 1 in 5 meetings closes and a deal is worth $5,000, then 14 ÷ 5 = 2.8 deals × $5,000 = $14,000 a month.

Now check how sensitive this is. The 8x gap was measured on inbound web leads, and your gap may be smaller. If slow replies only did half as well (20% instead of 40%): today is 20 × 40% + 40 × 20% = 8 + 8 = 16 meetings, vs 24 when all are fast. You still lose 8 meetings a month. Even with a small gap, speed is the cheapest meeting you'll ever get.

Measure your own gap: For one month, log each interested reply with two times: when it came in and when you answered. Split them into "under 5 minutes" and "slower" and compare how many booked. That's your real number.

Hand it over BEFORE the call

If someone else runs the call, "You've got a call with Mark at 10" isn't a handover. Without the story, the closer asks questions the prospect already answered by email, and the call starts cold. Send the full story, short, before the call.

Copy-paste: handover brief
CALL BRIEF: [first name last name], [title] at [company]
When: [day, date, time + their time zone]
Company: [what they do, size, location, website]

How we got here:
- Email they replied to: [step number + subject line]
- What the email offered: [one line]
- Their reply, word for word: "[paste]"
- Anything else they asked or said: [paste]

What we think they need: [one line, based on their reply and website]
Goal of this call: [e.g. agree on a proposal, book a demo with their team]
Watch out for: [e.g. already uses a competitor, asked about price]
Owner of this lead: [name]

Send it in the same place every time (your CRM note, a Slack channel or the calendar invite description) so the closer always knows where to look.

Common mistakes

  • Replying with a long pitch. They already said yes. Just book the time.
  • Offering a time next week. Interest fades. Go 1 to 2 working days out.
  • Answering from a different person or a new email thread, so they don't know who you are.
  • Asking "What time works for you?" with no suggestion. That's the calendar link problem again, in words.
  • Calling once and giving up. If you have their number, call, then send the email anyway.

Quick answers

Isn't a calendar link more polite? It lets them pick.

Some people do prefer a link, and that's fine: if they ask, send it (Template 2). For everyone else, one time is less work, and "if it doesn't fit, send me a better time" keeps it polite.

What if I don't know their time zone?

Check their company's location on their website or their email signature. If you still can't tell, write the time in two zones, like "Thursday at 10:00 am ET / 7:00 am PT".

I can't answer within 5 minutes at night. Is it too late?

No. Answer first thing the next working morning, before anything else. The speed targets are for working hours. The real rule is that no interested reply waits longer than it has to.

Should I call if they only replied by email?

If you have their number, a quick call works well: "Saw your reply, wanted to grab a time while you're around." If they don't pick up, send the one-time reply by email anyway, so they can answer in two seconds.
