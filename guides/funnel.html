The 4 numbers that run your funnel

  • Reply rate: replies ÷ emails sent.
  • Interested rate: interested replies ÷ all replies. Many replies are "no thanks" or out-of-office.
  • Call rate: booked calls ÷ interested replies.
  • Close rate: signed clients ÷ calls.

Multiply them together and you know how many emails one client costs. Change any one of them and the whole number moves.

  1. Pick your goal: how many new clients you want per month.
  2. Calls needed = clients ÷ close rate. (2 clients ÷ 0.2 = 10 calls)
  3. Interested replies needed = calls ÷ call rate. (10 ÷ 0.25 = 40)
  4. Replies needed = interested ÷ interested rate. (40 ÷ 0.15 = about 267)
  5. Emails needed = replies ÷ reply rate. (267 ÷ 0.03 = about 8,900 a month)
  6. Per working day = monthly emails ÷ 22. (about 405 a day, so 14 inboxes, see our MATH guide)
Check the money: Multiply clients by your average deal value. If 2 clients bring $6,000 and the setup costs a few hundred dollars a month, the math works. If your deal is $200, cold email needs a much better conversion rate to pay off.

Which lever to pull first

Every number multiplies the others, so improving any one of them by 2x halves the emails you need. But some are much easier to move than others.

LeverHow to improve itEffect
Who you emailTighter lists, real decision makers, 1–2 people per companyRaises reply and interested rate together. Biggest win.
First line and offerAbout them, one real result, small questionRaises reply rate
Speed of replyAnswer interested leads the same day, ideally within an hourRaises call rate, costs nothing
The askOffer something easy, like a short call or a free auditRaises call rate
The sales callPrepare for each call, follow up afterRaises close rate

Example: raise the reply rate in the plan above from 3% to 4.5% and you need about 5,900 emails a month instead of 8,900. That's 5 fewer inboxes to buy and warm up.

Quick answers

Why did the construction software campaign need so many emails per client?

It sold annual software contracts to construction companies, a careful buyer with a long sales cycle. Simpler offers with faster decisions usually need fewer emails per client.

How long before my own numbers are reliable?

Give it at least 2 to 4 weeks and a few thousand emails before you trust the rates. Small numbers jump around a lot.

What improves the math fastest?

Better targeting. Emailing the right decision makers raises replies, interested replies and calls at the same time, more than any copy trick.

Should I count out-of-office replies?

Count them as replies if your tool does, but never as interested. That's why the interested rate matters more than the reply rate.
