The short answer

A setup that sends about 1,000 cold emails a day costs about $357 a month in tools. That money buys inboxes, domains and a sending tool. It does not buy replies. Replies come from the offer, the list and the copy. Fix those first.

Where the $357 comes from

We used the numbers from our sending calculator: about 30 emails a day per inbox and 3 inboxes per domain. For 1,000 a day that is 34 inboxes on 12 domains. Prices below are from the official pricing pages, checked on November 21, 2026. Prices: Google Workspace Business Starter is $7 per user a month on the annual plan.   • Lists are not in the $357. You can start with free sources and your own research. Paid data tools add to the bill.

Why more tools rarely fix zero replies

Every tool on that list does one job: get the email into the inbox. If the email lands and nobody answers, a new tool changes nothing. The reader still sees the same offer, sent to the same people, in the same words.

A tool can't write what you offer.

The 4-step order

Step 1: The offer

Why it matters: the offer is the reason to say yes. If a stranger can't see what they get and why it's low risk, nothing else saves the email.

  1. Write what you do in one line: "We help [who] get [result] in [time]."
  2. Add one piece of proof: a number, a client type or a short result.
  3. Make the ask small: a 15-minute call, a free audit or a short sample.
  4. Test it: read it out loud. If a friend in that job wouldn't say "yes, send it", rewrite it.

Step 2: The list

Why it matters: the best email fails if it goes to people who can't buy. In the reel, 10 names became 4 after we removed people with no budget, no need or the wrong job title.

  1. Write down who signs the check: job title, company size, industry, country.
  2. Remove anyone who doesn't match, even if it makes the list smaller.
  3. Verify every email before you send.
  4. Keep your best 2 or 3 reasons why this company needs you now (hiring, new funding, new location).

Step 3: The copy

Why it matters: busy people decide in a few seconds. Short, plain emails with one ask are easy to answer.

  • 3 to 5 short lines.
  • Plain words. No "leverage" or "synergy".
  • One simple ask at the end.
  • No images, no attachments, no links in the first email.

Step 4: The tools

Why last: tools make a working campaign bigger. They can't make a broken one work. Once steps 1 to 3 get replies on a small test, buy the inboxes you need to scale.

Quick answers

Can I start for less than $357?

Yes. That number is for about 1,000 emails a day. A small test with a few inboxes on one or two domains costs much less. Start small, prove the offer, then grow.

Which tool is the best?

Any well-known sending tool with warm-up works. The tool matters far less than your offer, list and copy.

How do I know if my problem is the offer or the list?

Read your replies. "Not interested" from the right people points to the offer. "Wrong person" or "we don't do that" points to the list.
