Why "sounds interesting" isn't a yes

"Sounds interesting" usually means "I'm curious, but I'm not sure". If you book a call with everyone who says it, your calendar fills with people who won't buy for a year, and your real buyers wait.

The fix isn't to ask if they want to buy. Everyone says maybe. Ask when.

The 4 questions

Ask them in the reply thread, one at a time, in this order. Each one is short and easy to answer.

1. How are you handling it now?

Quick question so I don't waste your time: how are you handling new leads at the moment?

Why: it shows whether there's a real problem. "We have a team and it works fine" is very different from "honestly, it's just me and it's messy".

2. What would a good result look like?

If this worked, what would a good result look like for you? More calls, a new market, something else?

Why: it tells you what they really want, in their own words. You'll use those exact words on the call.

3. Who else is part of the decision?

Is this your call, or would anyone else want to be part of the conversation?

Why: if a partner or boss decides, you want them on the call, not hearing about it secondhand.

4. This quarter or later?

Is this something you'd want sorted this quarter, or later in the year?

Why one question at a time

  • Four questions in one message feels like a form. People skip it.
  • One question gets a quick answer and keeps the conversation going.
  • Each answer helps you write a better next question.

This also sets up a better call. Gong's analysis of 519,000 discovery calls found the most successful calls had 11 to 14 targeted questions spread through the conversation. These 4 short questions in the thread mean you start the call already knowing the basics, so you can go deeper.

Reply fast

A Harvard Business Review study of 1.25 million leads found companies that responded within an hour were nearly 7 times more likely to qualify the lead than those that waited longer. Answer interested replies the same day, ideally within the hour.

The come-back message

Most deals are lost by forgetting, not by a no. When someone says "later", write the date and their words in your CRM, then send this on that date:

Come-back message
Hi [first name],

Back in [month] you mentioned looking at [their goal, in their words] around now. Is it still on the list for this quarter?

Happy to pick it up whenever suits you.

[your name]

It works because it uses their own words and their own timing. It's a reminder, not a pitch.

Quick answers

Won't asking questions slow down booking calls?

A little, but it saves you hours of calls with people who won't buy. Ready buyers answer quickly and happily.

What if they won't answer the questions?

Offer a short call instead. Some people prefer to talk. If they ignore both, they weren't ready.

Should I ask all 4 every time?

No. If they say "we need this now, can we talk Tuesday?", book it. The questions are for unclear replies like "sounds interesting".

How do I remember to send the come-back message?

Put the date in your CRM or calendar the moment they say "later". A simple spreadsheet works too.
