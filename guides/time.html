The One-Time Reply: 5 Words That Beat Your Calendar Link

This is the full guide from the reel. When a lead replies "interested", don't send a calendar link. Send five words: "Does Thursday at 10am work?" Here is why it works, exactly how to do it, and copy-paste replies for every answer you'll get.

The 5 words

A lead replies to your cold email: "Sounds interesting, tell me more." Most people answer with a calendar link and "grab any time that works." Then nothing happens. The lead meant to book, opened the link, saw a grid of 40 empty slots, and closed the tab to do it later. Later never came.

Instead, offer one specific time: "Does Thursday at 10am work?" It is a yes-or-no question. They can answer it from their phone in two seconds, without opening their calendar app or your booking page.

The reply (copy this)
Hi [First name],

Great. Does [Thursday] at [10am] [their time zone] work for a 15-minute call?

If not, send me a time that's better and I'll make it work.

[Your name]

Why one specific time works (the data)

Gong Labs analyzed 304,174 sales emails and counted which ones got a meeting booked within 10 days. They sorted the asks into three types: a specific ask (a day and a time), an open-ended ask ("Do you have time next week?") and an interest ask ("Are you interested in learning more?").

Once a buyer was already in the sales process, the specific ask won clearly:

Type of ask (Gong)ExampleSuccess rate once in the sales process
Specific day and time"Are you available Tuesday at 4pm?"37%
Open-ended"Do you have time next week?"32%
Interest"Are you interested in learning more?"25%

Gong's explanation is simple: the specific time gives the buyer an anchor. They either say "yes, that works" or "that doesn't work, but 1pm is open." They don't have to scan a busy calendar looking for white space. Less work means a faster decision.

Pro tip: The catch: in the same study, Gong found the interest ask works best in the very first cold email, because strangers don't want to give away time. So ask for interest first. Offer the specific time only after they say yes.

Step by step

  1. Reply fast. Interest cools quickly. Aim to answer within the hour during your work day.
  2. Find their time zone. Check their signature, their LinkedIn location or the company's office address. If you can't tell, use the city of the company's head office.
  3. Pick one slot 1 to 2 business days out. Close enough that the interest is still warm, far enough that they likely have it free. Mid-morning or early afternoon in their time zone is a safe default.
  4. Write the time in their zone. "10am ET", not just "10am". If you're in different zones, convert it before you send (the Meeting Planner in the resources does this).
  5. Ask one question, with an easy way out. "Does Thursday at 10am ET work? If not, send me a better time." One option keeps it a yes-or-no. The way out keeps it polite.
  6. When they say yes, send the invite within minutes. Add a one-line agenda so the call feels real. (Our no-show guide has the reminder messages.)
  7. If they offer another time, take it. Don't negotiate. Send the invite for their time.

Copy-paste replies for every answer

5 reply templates
1) They said "interested" / "tell me more":
Great. Does [Thursday] at [10am] [zone] work for a 15-minute call? If not, send me a time that's better.

2) They said "send me more info first":
Sure, here's the short version: [2 lines on what you do + one result]. Easier to show you live. Does [Thursday] at [10am] [zone] work for 15 minutes?

3) They said "that time doesn't work":
No problem. What day and time works better for you? I'll send the invite.

4) They said "send me your calendar link":
Of course: [link]. Pick anything that suits you.

5) They said "maybe next month":
Makes sense. Want me to pencil in [Tuesday the 3rd] at [10am] [zone]? Easy to move if things change.
Pro tip: If they ask for your link, send it. The goal is to remove work for the buyer, not to hide the link.

Checklist before you hit send

  • They already said yes or showed interest (otherwise ask for interest, not time)
  • One specific day and time, not a list
  • Written in their time zone, with the zone
  • 1 to 2 business days out
  • An easy way out: "If not, send me a better time"
  • Under 40 words
  • Invite ready to send the moment they say yes

Quick answers

Should I put a specific time in my first cold email?

Usually not. Gong found that asking for interest works best with strangers. Offer a specific time after they reply and show interest.

Why only one time and not three?

One time keeps it a simple yes-or-no question. The line "If not, send me a better time" covers the case where it doesn't fit.

Is a calendar link ever fine?

Yes. If the lead asks for it, send it right away. Some people prefer to pick their own slot.

What if I don't know their time zone?

Check their signature, LinkedIn location or company address. If you still can't tell, use the head office city and write the zone so they can correct you.
