Why "wrong person" is good news

Most people get "I'm not the right person" and move on. But that person just told you the company is real, the email works and someone else owns the problem. They know who it is. You only have to ask.

The 4 steps

StepWhat you doWhy it works
1. AskReply with one short questionEasy to answer in 5 seconds
2. Say who sent youEmail the new person and name who pointed you thereA name they know makes it warm
3. Put it in a follow-upAsk in every sequence, not only when they replyEvery "no" can become a pointer

Step 1: Ask one question

Why it works: a short, polite question is easier to answer than to ignore. Keep it to one line and thank them.

Reply to "wrong person"
Thanks for letting me know, {{first_name}}.

Who's the right person for this at {{company}}?

[Your name]
Keep it short: Don't re-pitch. Don't ask for a meeting. One question only.

Step 2: Email the new person and say who sent you

Why it works: your email is no longer from a stranger. It mentions a colleague's name, so it gets read.

Email to the right person
Subject: {{referrer_first_name}} suggested I reach out

Hi {{first_name}},

{{referrer_first_name}} mentioned you're the right person for [topic] at {{company}}.

We help [type of company] get [result]. [One line of proof.]

Worth a quick 15-minute call to see if it fits?

[Your name]
Be honest: Only say "{{name}} suggested I reach out" if they really pointed you there. If they only said "not me", write "I asked {{name}} who handles this."

Step 3: Put the question in a follow-up

Why it works: many people never reply to say "wrong person". A follow-up that asks the question gives them an easy way to help.

Follow-up with the referral question
Hi {{first_name}},

If this isn't on your plate, who at {{company}} handles [topic]?

Happy to reach out to them instead.

[Your name]

Step 4: Tag it so you follow up

Why it works: referrals get lost in a busy inbox. A tag turns them into a list you can work every week. In Instantly you can change a lead's status from Unibox; most tools have something similar.

  1. Create a tag or status called "Pointed to someone".
  2. Add the new person as a new lead with a note: who pointed you there and when.
  3. Send the step 2 email within one business day.
  4. If the new person doesn't answer in 3 to 4 days, send one short follow-up.
  5. If it works, thank the person who pointed you there. It builds goodwill.

Worked example

Your campaign gets 20 "wrong person" replies in a month. You send the one-line question to all 20. If even 5 of them name someone, you get 5 new conversations that start with a colleague's name instead of a cold intro. That's 5 warm leads you would have lost.

Quick answers

Isn't it pushy to ask for a name?

Not if it's one polite line. Most people are happy to point you to the right person so they stop getting the email.

What if they give me a name but no email?

Find the person's work email with a lookup tool and verify it before you send.

Should I copy the person who referred me?

Only if they offer. Otherwise just mention their name in the email.
