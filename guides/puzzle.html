Why most campaigns only fix one piece

Most people spend all their time on the email copy. It's the part you can see. But copy is the last piece, not the first. A perfect email sent to the wrong people, from an inbox that lands in spam, about an offer nobody understands, books nothing.

Cold email still works when all four pieces fit. Instantly's 2026 benchmark report puts the average reply rate at 3.43%, with the top quarter at 5.5% or more. Instantly notes that the top group shows better targeting. That's a list piece, not a copy piece.

Piece 1: The offer

Why it comes first: the offer decides who you target and what you write. If a buyer can't repeat your offer in one sentence, no copy will save it.

One-sentence offer template
We help [type of company] get [one result they want] in [time frame], without [the thing they hate doing].

Example: We help IT service firms book 10 sales calls a month, without hiring an SDR.
  1. Fill in the template above.
  2. Read it to someone outside your business and ask them to repeat it back.
  3. If they can't, cut words until they can.
  4. Pass test: a stranger repeats it correctly after one read.

Piece 2: The list

Why: small, focused lists win. Hunter's 2026 report, based on 31 million emails, found sequences with 21 to 50 recipients got a 6.2% reply rate vs 2.4% for sequences with 500+ recipients. The smaller list let senders write for one type of buyer.

  1. Write down one buyer: industry, company size and job title.
  2. Build a list of only those people. Split anything different into its own campaign.
  3. Verify every email address before sending. Hunter's average bounce rate was 3.6%. Instantly says keep bounces under 2%.
  4. Pass test: you can describe everyone on the list in one sentence, and the list is verified.

Piece 3: The setup

Why: the setup decides inbox or spam. Google's sender guidelines require SPF or DKIM for all senders, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC for anyone sending 5,000+ messages a day to Gmail. Google also says to start with low volume and increase slowly.

Piece 4: The copy

Why it's last: once the offer, list and setup are right, copy is easy. You're writing to one type of person about one clear result. Instantly found top campaigns keep first emails under 80 words with a single call to action.

First email template (under 80 words)
Subject: [their company] + [result]

Hi [first name],

[One line that shows you know their world, e.g. "Most IT firms your size win clients through referrals."]

We help [type of company] get [result] in [time frame], without [pain].

[One short proof line, only if it's real.]

Worth a quick chat next week?

[Your name]
  1. Count the words. Under 80, or cut.
  2. One question at the end. No links, no attachments in email 1.
  3. Plan 4 to 7 emails in the sequence. Instantly found 42% of replies come from follow-ups.
  4. Pass test: a reader can tell who it's for and what they get within 5 seconds.
Order matters: Fix the pieces in order: offer, list, setup, copy. Each piece depends on the one before it.

Worked example: what a full picture can look like

Say you send to 1,000 verified buyers and hit Instantly's average reply rate of 3.43%.

  1. 1,000 × 3.43% = about 34 replies.
  2. In our full-quarter case study, about 1 in 4 replies came from a buyer who wanted to talk (1,061 of 4,372).
  3. 34 × 24% = about 8 interested buyers from 1,000 emails.
  4. Now break one piece. If the setup fails and half your emails land in spam, the same 1,000 emails reach only 500 inboxes, and replies fall to about 17.

The four-piece checklist

  • Offer: a stranger can repeat it in one sentence
  • List: one buyer type per campaign
  • List: every address verified, bounce target under 2%
  • Setup: separate sending domains, never the main domain

Quick answers

Which piece should I fix first?

The offer. It decides who goes on the list and what the email says. Then the list, then the setup, then the copy.

My copy is good but I get no replies. Why?

Usually the setup. If emails land in spam, nobody sees them. Check SPF, DKIM, DMARC and warm-up first.

How big should my list be?

Start small and focused. Hunter found sequences with 21 to 50 recipients got 6.2% replies vs 2.4% for 500+. Split big lists into small groups of one buyer type.
