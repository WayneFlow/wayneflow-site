The 14-Day Inbox Warm-Up Checklist: One Brick a Day Before You Send

This is the full warm-up checklist from the reel. A new inbox needs 14 days of steady warm-up before cold email goes out. Here's what to do each day, how to tell it's working, and how to ramp up after day 14.

Why a new inbox can't send on day one

A brand-new inbox has no history. Inbox providers don't know if it's a real person or a spammer. If it suddenly sends dozens of emails to strangers, it looks like a spammer, and your emails start going to spam.

Google's sender guidelines say it plainly: start with a low sending volume and increase it slowly over time, and avoid sudden volume spikes if you don't have a history of sending. Warm-up builds that history.

What warm-up is: a tool sends and receives normal-looking emails between your inbox and a network of other real inboxes. They get opened, replied to and moved out of spam. Over time, providers learn your inbox is real. Tools like Instantly and Smartlead do it automatically.

Before day 1: lay the foundation

  1. Use a separate sending domain, never your main business domain.
  2. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the domain. Our DNS records guide has the values.
  3. Fill in the inbox profile: real first and last name and a profile photo.
  4. Point the sending domain to your real website.
  5. Connect the inbox to your sending tool and turn warm-up on.

The 14-day brick plan

DaysWhat happensYour job
Day 1Warm-up starts with a few emails a dayCheck SPF, DKIM and DMARC pass in a DNS checker
Days 2–3Warm-up volume rises slowlySend 2–3 normal emails by hand to people you know
Days 4–7The wall is still short. This is normalDon't send cold email yet. Check warm-up emails land in the inbox
Days 8–10Steady daily activity builds historySet up Google Postmaster Tools for the domain
Days 11–14History looks normal and steadyCheck the warm-up health score in your tool
Day 15+The wall holdsStart cold email slowly. Keep warm-up on
Week one feels slow: Nothing exciting happens in week one. That's the point. Every steady day is a brick that holds up the next one.

After day 14: ramp up, don't jump

Day 14 is when you can start, not when you go full speed. Instantly's 2026 benchmark report recommends starting at 5 to 10 emails a day, then building up over 4 to 6 weeks.

Worked example: 10 new inboxes

  1. Weeks 1 and 2: warm-up only. 0 cold emails.
  2. Week 3: 10 inboxes × 10 = 100 cold emails a day.
  3. Week 4: 10 inboxes × 20 = 200 a day.
  4. Week 5: 10 inboxes × 30 = 300 a day.
  5. At 300 a day for 20 working days, that's 6,000 emails a month, built on a base that holds.

How to tell the wall is holding

  • Warm-up emails land in the main inbox, not spam or promotions
  • Your tool's warm-up health score is high and steady
  • Bounce rate stays under 2% once you start (Instantly's guidance)
  • Google Postmaster Tools spam rate stays under 0.3% (Google's limit)
  • Replies come in, not just silence

Daily 2-minute check during warm-up

  1. Open your sending tool and check each inbox's warm-up score.
  2. Look for any inbox with errors or a disconnect. Fix it the same day.
  3. After day 8, glance at Google Postmaster Tools.
  4. Write down the day number. Don't start cold email before day 15.

Bottom line: Fourteen days of patience protects months of sending. Lay one brick a day, then build slowly.

Quick answers

Can I send a few cold emails during warm-up?

We don't recommend it. Give the inbox the full 14 days first, then start at about 10 a day.

Do I need to turn warm-up off once I start sending?

No. Keep it on, always. It keeps steady, healthy activity on the inbox.

Is 14 days enough?

It's enough to start slowly. Instantly recommends building to full volume over 4 to 6 weeks, so keep ramping after day 14.
