The Cold Email Setup Checklist: Domains, DNS Records and a Two-Week Warm-Up

Why the foundation beats clever copy

The campaigns that win are rarely the ones with the cleverest copy. They're the ones whose emails actually reach the inbox. If your setup is weak, your best email lands in spam and nobody reads it.

Google's sender guidelines require SPF or DKIM for every sender, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC for anyone sending 5,000+ messages a day to Gmail. Google also asks senders to keep spam rates under 0.3% and to increase volume slowly.

Step 1: Separate sending domains

Why: if a sending domain gets flagged, you swap it out. If your main domain gets flagged, your invoices, proposals and client emails start landing in spam too. Custom domains also do better than free addresses: Hunter's 2026 report found 5.2% replies from custom domains vs 2.5% from free addresses like Gmail.

  1. Never send cold email from your main domain.
  2. Buy lookalike domains. For acme.com: getacme.com, acmehq.com, tryacme.com.
  3. Choose .com when you can.
  4. Forward each lookalike domain to your real website.
  5. Put no more than 3 inboxes on each domain, and keep 1 spare domain warming in the background.

Step 2: The right records

Why: these records prove the email really comes from you and wasn't changed on the way. Set all three on every sending domain, before warm-up starts.

RecordWhat it doesExample (Google Workspace)
SPFLists the servers allowed to send for your domainv=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
DKIMA signature that proves the email wasn't changedGenerate the key in your email admin panel, then add it as a TXT record
DMARCTells inboxes what to do when SPF or DKIM failsv=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourdomain.com
  1. Log in to your domain registrar's DNS settings.
  2. Add the SPF record as a TXT record. Use only one SPF record per domain.
  3. Generate DKIM in your email provider and add it. Google recommends a 2048-bit key if your provider supports it.
  4. Add the DMARC record. A policy of "none" is fine to start; Google allows it.
  5. Check all three with a free DNS checker. Our DNS records guide walks through every value.

Step 3: A two-week warm-up

Why: a new inbox has no history. Google says to start with low volume and avoid sudden spikes. Warm-up tools like Instantly send and receive normal-looking emails with other real inboxes, so providers learn your inbox is real.

  1. Turn warm-up on for every new inbox on day 1.
  2. Send zero cold emails for 14 days.
  3. From day 15, start at about 10 cold emails per inbox per day.
  4. Add 10 a week until you reach about 30. Instantly's 2026 report recommends building up over 4 to 6 weeks.
  5. Keep warm-up on forever, even at full volume.

Worked example: setup for 400 emails a day

  1. 400 ÷ 30 emails per inbox = 13.3, so round up to 14 inboxes.
  2. 14 ÷ 3 inboxes per domain = 4.7, so round up to 5 domains (room for 15 inboxes).
  3. Add 1 spare domain warming in the background.
  4. Weeks 1–2: warm-up only. Week 3: 14 × 10 = 140 a day. Week 4: 14 × 20 = 280 a day. Week 5: 14 × 30 = 420 a day, a little above the goal.

The full setup checklist

  • Main domain never used for cold email
  • Lookalike .com domains bought and forwarded to the real website
  • Max 3 inboxes per domain, plus 1 spare domain
  • One SPF record per domain
  • DKIM key generated and added (2048-bit if possible)
  • DMARC record added (p=none is fine to start)
  • All records checked in a DNS checker
  • Google Postmaster Tools set up for each domain
  • 14 days of warm-up before the first cold email
  • Ramp 10 → 20 → 30 per inbox per day, warm-up always on

Quick answers

How many domains do I need?

Divide your daily goal by 30 to get inboxes, then divide by 3 to get domains. Round up both times and add 1 spare domain.

Is DMARC with p=none good enough?

Yes, to start. Google's guidelines say the DMARC policy can be set to none. What matters is that the record exists and SPF or DKIM passes.

Why two weeks of warm-up?

A new inbox has no history. Two weeks of steady, normal activity gives providers a reason to trust it before cold email starts.
