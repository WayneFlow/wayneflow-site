The short answer

Send about 30 cold emails a day from each inbox, and put no more than 3 inboxes on each domain. Then add inboxes until you reach your daily goal. That's the whole idea. The rest of this guide is the detail that makes it work.

The time to full volume is the same at every size: 2 weeks of warm-up, then a 3-week ramp. Buying more inboxes doesn't make it faster. It only makes the final number bigger.

Why 30 a day per inbox

Google Workspace lets a paid inbox send up to 2,000 emails a day (only 500 on a trial account), per Google's own limits page. That's a system limit, not a safe cold email limit. Inbox providers judge each inbox by how it behaves, and a brand-new inbox sending hundreds of emails to strangers looks exactly like a spammer.

The data agrees with keeping it low. In Hunter's 2026 report (31 million emails sent in 2025), inboxes sending 20 to 49 emails a day got a 5.7% reply rate, above the 4.5% average. 30 a day sits safely in that range and is easy to do the math with.

Simple rule: Need more volume? Add more inboxes. Never push one inbox harder.

Why 3 inboxes per domain

  • It spreads the risk. If one domain gets a bad reputation, you lose 3 inboxes, not your whole setup.
  • It keeps each domain's volume low. 3 inboxes × 30 = about 90 emails a day per domain, which looks like a normal small team.
  • It makes problems easy to find. When results drop, you can see which domain is the problem and pause only that one.

Step 1: Buy the right domains

  1. Never send cold email from your main domain. If it gets flagged, your normal emails to clients and invoices start going to spam too.
  2. Buy domains that look like your brand: for acme.com, try getacme.com, acmehq.com, tryacme.com or acme-team.com.
  3. Stick to .com where you can. Unusual endings look less trustworthy to people and filters.
  4. Point every sending domain to your real website, so anyone who checks it lands somewhere real.
  5. Buy 1 or 2 spare domains and warm them up too. When one gets tired, you swap it in without waiting 5 weeks.

Step 2: Set up the DNS records

Every sending domain needs three records before it sends anything: SPF (which servers can send for you), DKIM (a signature that proves the email wasn't changed) and DMARC (what to do with emails that fail the first two). Our DNS records guide shows the exact values to paste.

These aren't optional anymore. Google's sender rules require them, and anyone who sends about 5,000 or more emails a day to Gmail addresses counts as a bulk sender with stricter rules. That status is counted per main domain and doesn't go away if your volume drops later. Yahoo and Microsoft (Outlook.com) have similar rules.

Step 3: Warm up, then ramp

Warm-up means the inbox sends and receives normal-looking emails with other inboxes for a while, so providers learn it's a real sender. Most sending tools (Instantly, Smartlead and others) do this for you automatically.

This plan puts you at full volume in week 5. That matches Instantly's 2026 benchmark report, which says new domains need 4 to 6 weeks before full volume. Instantly also found that steady sending gets 15 to 20% more replies than on-and-off sending, so send a similar amount every working day instead of big bursts.

Don't turn warm-up off: Leave warm-up running even after you reach full volume. It keeps a steady stream of good activity on every inbox.

Do this every Monday. It takes 10 minutes and catches problems before they cost you a domain.

Set up Google Postmaster Tools for each sending domain. It's free, and it's the only place Google shows you how it sees your domain. You may need some volume before data shows up.

Worked example: 1,000 emails a day

  1. 1,000 ÷ 30 = 33.3, so round up to 34 inboxes.
  2. 34 ÷ 3 = 11.3, so round up to 12 domains (that gives you room for 36 inboxes).
  3. Add 2 spare domains with 3 inboxes each, warmed up and ready.
  4. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on all 14 domains on day 1.
  5. Weeks 1–2: warm-up only. Week 3: 340 a day. Week 4: 680 a day. Week 5: 1,020 a day.
  6. From week 5, run the Monday health check and swap a spare domain in if one goes red.

The 6 mistakes that burn domains

  • Sending from the main business domain.
  • Skipping warm-up because "the domain is new, so it's clean". New is exactly the problem.
  • Jumping from 0 to full volume in week one.
  • Sending to a list you never verified, so bounces shoot past 2%.
  • Sending big bursts on some days and nothing on others.
  • Ignoring the numbers until replies suddenly stop.

Quick answers

Can I send more than 30 a day per inbox?

Some senders push older, healthy inboxes higher. It raises the risk, and in Hunter's data the 20 to 49 range did best. Adding inboxes is the safer way to grow.

Can I use free Gmail accounts instead?

Don't. Free accounts have lower limits, and Hunter found emails from free addresses got 2.5% replies vs 5.2% from custom domains.

How much does this setup cost?

You pay per domain each year, per inbox each month and for a sending tool. Prices change often, so compare current prices on each provider's site before you buy.

Does the 5,000-a-day bulk sender rule apply to me?

It counts emails to Gmail addresses from your main domain in one day. Even below it, the same rules (authentication, low spam rate, easy opt-out) are what keep you out of spam, so follow them anyway.
