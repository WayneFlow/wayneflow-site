Why bursts don't work

Most people send cold email in bursts. They write a batch, hit send, then forget about it for a week. That creates two problems. First, your pipeline goes up and down: a busy week of replies, then nothing. Second, sudden spikes in volume look bad to inbox providers. Google's sender guidelines tell senders to increase volume slowly and avoid sudden jumps.

A belt fixes both. A steady number of emails goes out every day, so replies come in every day. You build it once, then it keeps moving while you do your real work.

Step 1: Do the daily math

Why: you can't build a belt without knowing how much it needs to carry each day. Start from your monthly target and work backward.

Daily sending formula
Emails per month ÷ sending days per month = emails per day
Emails per day ÷ emails per inbox per day = inboxes you need

Example from the reel: 12,000 ÷ 30 = 400 emails a day.
  1. Pick a monthly number you can keep up for months, not weeks.
  2. Decide if you send every day or weekdays only. 12,000 over 30 days is 400 a day. Over about 22 weekdays it's about 545 a day.
  3. Pick a low number per inbox and divide. Example: 400 a day ÷ 20 per inbox = 20 inboxes.
  4. Pass test: you can say your daily number and how many inboxes carry it.

Step 2: Build the belt (the setup)

Why: the belt only moves if emails reach the inbox. That depends on the setup, not the copy.

  1. Buy separate sending domains. Never send cold email from your main business domain.
  2. Set SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain. Google requires these for bulk senders. Our DNS records guide has the values.
  3. Create the inboxes you calculated in Step 1. Stay well under the provider's daily limits: Google Workspace caps a user at 2,000 messages a day, and cold email should run far below that.
  4. Warm up every new inbox for at least 2 weeks before the first cold email, then ramp up slowly.
  5. Pass test: all records check out in a DNS checker and every inbox has finished warm-up.

Step 3: Load the belt (the list)

Why: a belt that runs out of leads stops. At 400 a day you need about 12,000 leads a month, counting only first emails.

  1. Build at least one month of verified leads before you start.
  2. Add new leads every week so the belt never runs empty.
  3. Keep one buyer type per campaign. Hunter found small, focused sequences got a 6.2% reply rate vs a 4.5% average.
  4. Verify every address so bounces don't damage your inboxes.

Step 4: Set the schedule and the follow-ups

Why: follow-ups are a big part of the belt. Instantly's 2026 benchmark report found 42% of replies come from follow-up emails, not the first one.

  1. Set a sending window in your tool, e.g. weekdays during the buyer's business hours.
  2. Set a daily limit per inbox so the tool spreads emails out evenly.
  3. Write a sequence of 4 to 7 emails with a few days between each step.
  4. Turn on auto-stop when someone replies, so nobody gets a follow-up after answering.
Remember: The belt includes follow-ups. Your daily number covers first emails and follow-ups together, so plan inbox capacity for both.

Step 5: Check the belt daily (10 minutes)

  • Did today's emails go out? Check the sent count against your daily number.
  • Any bounces? Keep the bounce rate low. If it climbs, pause and verify the list.
  • Any inbox with errors or disconnected? Fix it the same day.
  • Any replies? Answer interested buyers fast.
  • Leads left in the campaign: at least 2 weeks' worth?

Worked example: what a steady belt carries

Say your belt sends 400 emails a day for 30 days, which is 12,000 emails a month.

  1. At Instantly's average reply rate of 3.43%: 12,000 × 3.43% = about 412 replies a month.
  2. That's about 412 ÷ 30 = 14 replies a day, landing while you work.
  3. In our full-quarter case study, about 1 in 4 replies came from a buyer who wanted to talk (1,061 of 4,372).
  4. 412 × 24% = about 99 interested buyers a month, if your results match those averages.

Quick answers

Can I just send more emails from one inbox?

No. Spread the volume across several inboxes and keep each one low. Big volume from one inbox is how you end up in spam.

Should I send on weekends?

Most B2B senders stick to weekdays, when buyers read work email. If you do, your daily number goes up, since the same monthly total is spread over fewer days.

How long before the belt is ready?

Plan for about two weeks of warm-up after your domains and inboxes are set up. Use that time to build and verify your first month of leads.
