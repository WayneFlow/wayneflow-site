The short answer

Your Monday has 0 calls. The company in the reel, a home services business that used to rely on Facebook ads, booked about 29 calls a month with cold email. That's about 7 a week. The gap isn't luck. It's three things done in order:

  1. A list of people who fit. The right companies and the right person at each one.
  2. A setup that lands in the inbox. Separate domains, authenticated, warmed up.
  3. Volume, every day. The same amount of new people reached every working day, with follow-ups.

Done in order, this takes about 6 weeks from zero to full volume: 2 weeks of building and warm-up, a 3-week ramp, then steady sending. The checklist below covers each week.

Step 1: A list of people who fit

Why it matters: nobody outside your list can ever book a call. And a list of people who don't fit gets replies like "not interested" or no replies at all, which also hurts your sending reputation. Fit beats size.

Exactly how: write the one-sentence buyer description, pull companies that match, find 1 or 2 decision makers per company, verify every email, and note 2 real details about each company. Details matter: in Hunter's State of Email Outreach 2026, emails with two custom details got 5.6% replies vs 3.6% without.

Step 2: A setup that lands in the inbox

Why it matters: an email in spam is an email nobody reads. The big providers now check every sender. Google requires SPF or DKIM from everyone, all three (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) from bulk senders, and a spam rate below 0.3% (it recommends under 0.1%). Microsoft requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC for domains sending over 5,000 emails a day to Outlook.com.

Exactly how: separate sending domains, up to 3 inboxes per domain, the three DNS records on each, and warm-up turned on before any cold email goes out. Instantly's 2026 benchmark report says to start at 5 to 10 emails a day and build up over 4 to 6 weeks.

Step 3: Volume, every day

Why it matters: one big send fills one week. Steady sending fills every week. Instantly found that teams who send consistently get 15 to 20% more replies, and that bursty sending (500 one day, nothing for three days, then 1,000) looks suspicious to providers. Follow-ups matter too: 42% of replies in Instantly's data came after the first email.

Exactly how: the same daily limit on every inbox, Monday to Friday. New people added every day. Every person gets 3 emails, 3 to 4 days apart.

Week 0: The list

  • Write your buyer sentence: "[Job title] at [type of company] with [size] in [place]."
  • List 3 problems this buyer has that you solve. Pick the biggest one for email 1.
  • Pull matching companies from a lead database, Google Maps or industry directories.
  • Find 1 or 2 decision makers per company with work emails.
  • Verify every email. Remove anything that's invalid or risky.
  • Add 2 real details per company in your sheet (what they do, a recent change, where they work).
  • Check the count against your goal (see the worked example below).

Week 2: Warm-up and the emails

  • Leave warm-up running. Send zero cold emails this week.
  • Write the 3-email sequence (use the prompt below). Email 1 under 80 words.
  • Add a plain way to say no to each email, like "Not for you? Reply no and I won't email again."
  • Remove links and images from email 1.
  • Send the sequence to yourself on Gmail and Outlook. Read it on your phone.
  • Set the schedule: Monday to Friday, during your buyers' work hours, 3 to 4 days between emails.

Week 3 and 4: The ramp

  • Week 3: 10 cold emails per inbox per day. Week 4: 20 per inbox per day.
  • Launch new people on Monday. Instantly's data shows Monday is a good day to start sequences and Wednesday gets the most replies.
  • Check bounces every day. Over 2%? Stop that list and verify it again.
  • Answer every interested reply within a few hours. Offer one specific time.
  • Log every reply: interested, not now, wrong person, no.

Week 5 and after: Full volume

  • 30 cold emails per inbox per day, every weekday. Keep warm-up on.
  • Add new people every day so first emails never stop.
  • Order the next list when the current one is 75% used.
  • Run the Monday routine below, every week.

Worked example: from 0 to 5 calls a week

Say your goal is 5 booked calls a week (about 22 a month: 5 × 4.33 = 21.7). Use a starting guess of 2 calls per 1,000 people. Here's where it comes from: Hunter's average is 45 replies per 1,000 people (4.5%). In our full-quarter case study about 1 in 4 replies was interested, so about 11. In our 5-day blitz about 1 in 5 interested people booked (25 of 115), so about 2.

  1. New people needed each week: 5 calls ÷ 2 per 1,000 = 2,500 people a week.
  2. Each gets 3 emails: 2,500 × 3 = 7,500 emails a week once you're at full volume.
  3. Per working day: 7,500 ÷ 5 = 1,500 emails a day.
  4. Inboxes at 30 a day each: 1,500 ÷ 30 = 50 inboxes.
  5. Domains at 3 inboxes each: 50 ÷ 3 = 16.7, so 17 domains, plus 2 spares = 19.
  6. List for the first month: 2,500 × 4 weeks = 10,000 verified people, so build at least 2,000 to 3,000 in week 0 and keep adding.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping week 0. Buying inboxes before you know who you're emailing.
  • Sending in week 1. New domains need warm-up first. Rushing burns them.
  • Stopping after email 1. You lose the 42% of replies that come from follow-ups.
  • Sending only when you have time. Steady beats bursts, in replies and in deliverability.
  • Judging it in week 3. The ramp is not full volume. Judge results from week 6 on.

Quick answers

Can I skip the warm-up if I'm in a hurry?

Don't. New domains that send lots of cold email right away look like spammers. Instantly says to build up over 4 to 6 weeks. Skipping it can cost you the domains and the weeks anyway.

How many people do I need on my list to start?

Enough for the ramp plus a buffer. In the worked example, that's 2,500 new people a week at full volume. Start with at least 2,000 to 3,000 verified people and keep adding every week.

Why Monday?

It's a natural start for the week. Instantly's data shows Monday is a good day to launch new sequences and Wednesday gets the most replies. Use Monday for your routine and new batches.

What if I only want 1 or 2 calls a week?

Use the same steps with smaller numbers. At 2 calls per 1,000 people, 2 calls a week is about 1,000 new people a week, 3,000 emails a week, 600 a day and 20 inboxes.
