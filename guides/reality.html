Expectation vs reality

Expectation: buy a sending tool, load 10,000 contacts, press send, watch the replies come in. Reality: one inbox sending 10,000 emails gets flagged the same day, and every email lands in spam.

Inbox providers judge every inbox by how it behaves. A real person doesn't send thousands of emails a day to strangers, so an inbox that does gets treated like a spammer. Google allows a paid Workspace inbox up to 2,000 emails a day (Google's limits page), but that's a system limit, not a safe cold email limit.

The calculator

Two rules run everything: about 30 cold emails a day per inbox, and 3 inboxes per domain. In Hunter's 2026 report, inboxes sending 20 to 49 emails a day got a 5.7% reply rate, above the 4.5% average, so 30 sits in the safe zone.

The number nobody talks about: leads

Volume isn't only about inboxes. You also need enough new people to email. Most of your daily emails are follow-ups to people already in a sequence, so the list you need depends on how many emails each person gets.

  1. Emails per month = emails a day × 22 working days. (10,000 × 22 = 220,000)
  2. New contacts per month = emails per month ÷ emails per person. With a 4-email sequence: 220,000 ÷ 4 = 55,000 new contacts a month.
  3. Add about 20% extra, because verification removes bad addresses. That's roughly 66,000 raw contacts a month.
  4. Ask yourself: are there 66,000 new people every month who are real buyers for you? If not, a smaller volume aimed better will make more money.
Reality check: Most businesses don't have 55,000 good new prospects a month. In Hunter's data, small groups of 21 to 50 people got 6.2% replies vs 2.4% for 500+. Send what your market can take, not what your tool can send.

  1. Buy separate domains that look like your brand (for acme.com: getacme.com, tryacme.com, acmehq.com). Never send cold email from your main domain.
  2. Point each domain to your real website, so anyone who checks it lands somewhere real.
  3. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every domain. Our DNS records guide has the exact values.
  4. Create 3 inboxes per domain with real names and profile photos.
  5. Turn on warm-up for 14 days before any cold email. Aim for a warm-up health score of 99 or higher, and never switch warm-up off, even later.
  6. Ramp slowly: 10 a day per inbox in week 3, 20 in week 4, 30 from week 5.

The rules that apply at this size

Google's sender guidelines treat anyone sending about 5,000 or more emails a day to Gmail addresses as a bulk sender. That's counted per main domain, and the status doesn't go away later. Bulk senders must have SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up, keep spam reports under 0.3% (aim for under 0.1%), and make it easy to opt out. Yahoo and Microsoft have similar rules. Follow them at any volume.

Mistakes that cost the most at scale

  • Buying 300 inboxes before you've proven one campaign works at 500 a day.
  • Running out of good leads and filling the gap with a worse list.
  • No spare domains, so one burned domain stops a whole campaign for 5 weeks.
  • Checking health once a month instead of every day.
  • Sending to unverified lists because "it's only a few bounces". At 10,000 a day, 3% is 300 bounces a day.

Quick answers

Do I really need 334 inboxes for 10,000 a day?

At 30 a day per inbox, yes. Pushing each inbox harder needs fewer inboxes but raises the risk that whole domains land in spam.

Should I start at 10,000 a day?

No. Prove your list and email at a few hundred a day first. Scale only what already gets replies.

Can I use Google and Microsoft inboxes together?

Yes, many senders mix them so one provider's problem doesn't stop everything. Follow the same per-inbox limits on both.

What does this cost?

You pay per domain each year, per inbox each month and for a sending tool. Multiply by the numbers above and compare current prices on each provider's site.
