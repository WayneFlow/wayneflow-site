The short answer

When someone fills in your form, they're still at their desk, still thinking about the problem, often still on your site. Reply while they're still looking. Set up three things: route every form instantly to one owner, alert them within 5 minutes, and reply with a booking link. That's the whole playbook.

Part 1: Instant routing

Routing means every new form goes straight to the right person, the second it arrives, with everything they need to reply. No shared inbox that nobody checks.

  1. Pick one owner per lead. One name, not "the team". If you have several people, decide the rule now: by region, by deal size, or simple rotation.
  2. Connect your form to your CRM. Most form tools and CRMs (for example HubSpot) can create a contact automatically. If yours can't, use Zapier or Make: trigger = new form submission, action = create a contact.
  3. Send the lead to the owner's phone, not just their inbox. Add a second action that posts to a Slack channel or sends a text/push alert with name, company, email, phone and what they asked for.
  4. Assign it automatically. The lead should land with the owner's name on it, so nobody has to ask "who's taking this?"
  5. Test it. Fill in your own form with a test name and time how long the alert takes. It should be seconds.

Part 2: The 5-minute alert

The first alert tells the owner a lead is in. The 5-minute alert makes sure it never gets forgotten.

  1. Alert 1 (instant): "New lead: [name], [company]. Reply in 5 min." Sent to the owner's phone.
  2. Wait 5 minutes. In Zapier use a Delay step, in Make a Sleep step, or use your CRM's workflow delay.
  3. Check: has the lead been marked "contacted" (or has an email been logged)?
  4. If not, Alert 2 (escalation): send it again to the owner and to a backup person: "Still waiting: [name]. Who's got it?"
  5. After hours: send an instant automatic email that says when a human will reply, with the booking link, so they can book while they're still interested.
This won't work unless: ONE person owns that alert during working hours. An alert sent to five people is an alert everyone thinks someone else handled.

Part 3: The first reply

The first reply should be short, personal and make booking take one click. Write it once, save it as a template, and edit one line for each lead.

First-reply email template
Subject: Re: your request, [first name]

Hi [first name],

Thanks for reaching out about [what they asked for, in their words]. Got it.

Quick question so I can help properly: [one simple question, e.g. "Is this for your team of about X, or the whole company?"]

If it's easier to talk it through, grab any 15-minute slot here: [booking link]

Or just reply here, I'm on email today.

[Your name]
[Phone number]
Text / voicemail version (if they left a phone number)
Hi [first name], it's [your name] from [company]. You just asked about [topic] on our site. Happy to help. I sent you an email with a link to book 15 minutes, or call me back on [number] any time today.
  • Use their words from the form in the first line, so it's clearly not a robot.
  • One question only. It's easy to answer from a phone.
  • Booking link always. Use a tool like Calendly or your CRM's meeting link, with 15-minute slots for the next few days.
  • Call too, if they gave a number. Only 31% of companies in the Workato test called at all, so a quick call stands out.

Track your real response time

Every Monday, list last week's leads with two times: when the form came in and when the first personal reply went out. Work out the average. Your goal: most leads under 5 minutes in working hours, and every lead within an hour, the window HBR linked to nearly 7x better qualification.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the automatic "thanks, we got your message" email as the reply. It's not. A person still needs to answer.
  • Sending leads to a shared inbox like info@ that nobody owns.
  • Alerts only on email, which people check a few times a day.
  • Asking 5 questions in the first reply, so the lead has to write an essay.
  • No booking link, so a simple "yes" turns into three emails about times.

Quick answers

What if a lead comes in at 11 pm?

Send an instant automatic email with your booking link and say when a person will reply. Then make that lead the first reply of the next morning.

I'm a one-person business. Can I still do this?

Yes. Route every lead to your phone, keep the template saved, and reply when you can during the day. Even replying within an hour beats most companies in the Workato test.

Should I call or email first?

Do both if they left a number: a quick call, then the email with the booking link. If there's no number, email.

Does this apply to cold email replies too?

Yes. When a cold prospect replies "interested", they're a hot lead. Answer within minutes with a booking link, the same way.
