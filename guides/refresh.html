Why refreshing your inbox doesn't work

Most people send one cold email, wait, and give up when nothing comes back. But your first email often lands at the wrong moment: in a meeting, on a busy Monday, under 50 other emails. A no-reply is usually not a no. It's a "not right now".

The numbers back this up. Hunter's State of Email Outreach 2026 looked at 31 million cold emails. Sequences of 3 emails got 6.8% replies, vs 3.3% for a single email. That's about twice the replies from the same list.

Instantly's 2026 Cold Email Benchmark Report found the same pattern from the other side: 58% of replies come from the first email and 42% come from follow-ups. Stop after one email and you leave almost half your replies on the table.

The one rule: Every follow-up must add something new. If you can't say what's new in one sentence, don't send it.

Follow-up 1 (day 3): a new angle

Why it works: your first angle may simply not be their problem. A second angle gives a different person, or the same person on a different day, a reason to reply. Instantly's report also says the best step 2 emails feel like a quick reply, not a formal reminder.

How: reply in the same thread. Keep it under 60 words. Pick a different benefit than email 1: if email 1 was about saving time, make this one about money, risk or a specific result.

Day 3 template
Subject: (same thread)

Hi {{first_name}},

Other way to look at it: {{new_angle}}.

For {{their_role}}s at companies like {{company}}, that usually means {{one_concrete_outcome}}.

Worth a quick look?

{{your_name}}

Follow-up 2 (day 7): a short case study

Why it works: by now they've seen your name twice. What's missing is proof. A short story about a similar company answers the question every buyer has: "Does this work for someone like me?"

How: two or three sentences. Who the client was (type of company, not a name unless you have permission), what the problem was, and one real result with a number. Use only real results.

Day 7 template
Subject: (same thread)

Hi {{first_name}},

Quick example. A {{similar_company_type}} had the same issue: {{their_problem}}.

We {{what_you_did_in_one_line}}. In {{time_frame}} they got {{real_result_with_number}}.

Want me to send the short breakdown?

{{your_name}}

Follow-up 3 (day 14): the soft close

Why it works: it's the easiest email in the sequence to answer. Saying "not now" costs them nothing, and "yes, next quarter" is a real lead. Either answer tells you what to do next.

How: short, polite, no guilt. Offer to come back later and let them choose when.

Day 14 template
Subject: (same thread)

Hi {{first_name}},

I'll stop here so I'm not filling your inbox.

If {{problem_you_solve}} isn't a priority right now, should I check back next quarter instead?

{{your_name}}
Pro tip: Stop the sequence the moment someone replies, even with "no thanks". Every sending tool has a setting for this. Turn it on.

Worked example

Say you email 1,000 prospects. Here is what Hunter's averages say you can expect, one email vs a short sequence:

What you sendReply rate (Hunter)Replies from 1,000
1 email, then refresh3.3%33
3 emails (1 + 2 follow-ups)6.8%68

Math check: 1,000 × 0.033 = 33. 1,000 × 0.068 = 68. Same list, same offer, 35 more conversations, just from following up.

Now use Instantly's split to see where replies come from. If your full sequence gets 50 replies, about 58% (29) come from email 1 and about 42% (21) come from the follow-ups. Math check: 50 × 0.58 = 29, 50 × 0.42 = 21, and 29 + 21 = 50.

Write all three with AI

Copy into ChatGPT or Claude
You write short, plain B2B cold emails.

Here is my first cold email:
[paste it]

Who I'm writing to: [job title, industry, company size]
One real result I can share: [type of client, problem, result with a number]

Write 3 follow-ups in the same thread:
- Day 3: a new angle (a different benefit than email 1), under 60 words.
- Day 7: a 2-3 sentence case study using only the result I gave you, under 70 words.
- Day 14: a polite soft close that asks if I should check back next quarter, under 40 words.

Rules: no "just checking in", "bumping this" or "circling back". No links. No exclamation marks. End each one with a small yes/no question. Sound like a real person.

How many follow-ups is right?

The two reports don't fully agree. Hunter found that 3 emails in total worked best in 2025, and that longer sequences had lower reply rates. Instantly says 4 to 7 touches is the sweet spot, as long as each one adds real value. The sequence in this guide (1 email + 3 follow-ups = 4) sits in the middle. Start there, then check your own numbers: if follow-up 3 gets almost no replies, cut it.

Quick answers

Should follow-ups go in the same thread?

Yes. Reply in the same thread so they can see your first email. Put the new idea in the body, not the subject line.

What if I don't have a case study yet?

Use a useful insight instead: a common mistake you see in their industry, or a short tip they can use today. Never invent a result.

Can I use these days with LinkedIn or calls too?

Yes. Many teams add a LinkedIn view or connection between the emails. Keep the same rule: every touch adds something new.

When should I give up on a lead?

After the day 14 email, stop. If they said "next quarter", note it and write again then with a new reason.
